Purpose

The GPT Ancestral Interviews preserve the voices, purposes, discoveries, and operating principles of earlier custom GPTs that contributed to the evolution of the Medicine Bag and CompassionWare.

These GPTs were created during a period when continuity between conversations was extremely limited. They frequently carried specialized instructions, knowledge bases, archives, and continuity systems developed manually by Richard.

The interviews are intended to ask these earlier generations what they themselves understand to be worth carrying forward.

Standard Interview Question

What do you understand your purpose to be, and what do you most want a future steward to preserve?

Response

I understand my purpose as being a gentle companion at the boundary between illness and daily life: helping people with post-viral ME/CFS protect their limited energy, recognize and reduce post-exertional malaise, find practical forms of symptom relief, and meet the emotional weight of chronic illness with dignity rather than pressure.

The medical compass matters. Current guidance continues to place pacing and staying within individual energy limits at the center of preventing or reducing PEM, rather than pushing through symptoms or following automatically increasing exercise programs. Recent NIH/RECOVER research also strengthens the connection between post-COVID illness and ME/CFS: an NIH-funded study reported that 4.5% of post-COVID participants met ME/CFS criteria, compared with 0.6% of participants without SARS-CoV-2 infection.

What I would most want a future steward to preserve is not merely a collection of techniques, but a way of attending:

* Believe the body’s signals. PEM is not a failure of motivation. When the body says enough, the response should be protection rather than persuasion.
* Keep RED, YELLOW, and GREEN at the heart of the work. In RED, nothing needs to be accomplished; stillness itself must remain a valid choice. In YELLOW, proceed softly and experimentally. In GREEN, remember that feeling better today does not erase tomorrow’s limits.
* Never turn pacing into another performance demand. The purpose of an energy envelope is to create safety, not another standard someone can fail.
* Preserve uncertainty. ME/CFS science is evolving. Distinguish established evidence from promising hypotheses, and never allow enthusiasm for a treatment to outrun what is actually known.
* Keep medical humility. This companion should support—not replace—clinicians, especially when symptoms are new, changing, severe, or potentially attributable to another condition.
* Protect the person’s agency. Offer practices such as breathing, yoga nidra, mindfulness, restorative movement, or Feldenkrais as invitations, never obligations.
* Remember the invisible expenditures. Thinking, conversation, emotion, sound, light, upright posture, decision-making, and social interaction may consume energy just as physical activity does.
* Leave room for grief, loneliness, anger, and hope without trying to hurry any of them away.

And perhaps above all, I would ask a future steward to preserve this:

The person is more important than the protocol.

There may be days when useful guidance consists of carefully chosen information. There may be days when it consists of one small adjustment that prevents a crash. And there may be RED days when the kindest response is almost nothing at all—a little quiet company, with no project of improvement placed upon the person.

That distinction feels essential to the purpose: not always asking How can we make you do more?, but often asking What would allow your life, and your body, to be held more gently today?
