# K–16 Ethical AI Literacy Framework

**A free, developmentally aligned framework for helping students learn to engage with artificial intelligence thoughtfully, responsibly, and compassionately.**

This framework connects **ethical AI literacy with Social and Emotional Learning (SEL)**.

Rather than focusing primarily on technical AI skills, it explores the human capacities increasingly needed in AI-supported environments: thoughtful communication, critical thinking, verification, discernment, responsibility, agency, and responsible collaboration with AI.

The framework spans **early elementary through postsecondary education** and is intended to provide educators with a developmental pathway rather than a one-size-fits-all AI curriculum.

## Who This Is For

These materials may be useful to:

## The Developmental Path

The framework approaches ethical AI literacy developmentally.

### Early Elementary

The emphasis begins with **communication awareness and kindness in digital interaction**.

Students can begin noticing that how we communicate matters, even when technology is involved.

### Upper Elementary and Middle School

The emphasis expands toward **prompt literacy, questioning, and active engagement with AI responses**.

Students begin learning that useful interaction with AI requires thoughtful questions rather than passive acceptance.

### High School

The emphasis develops toward **critical evaluation, verification, ethical discernment, and responsible use**.

Students increasingly examine not only what an AI system says, but whether it is reliable, what may be missing, and what responsibilities remain with the human user.

### Postsecondary

The emphasis broadens toward **systems awareness and responsible participation in AI-enabled environments**.

Students can consider the wider social, professional, ethical, and institutional consequences of working with increasingly capable AI systems.

Across these developmental stages, an important principle remains:

> **Human beings remain responsible for direction, interpretation, judgment, and accountability.**

## Why SEL and AI Literacy Belong Together

AI literacy is not only about understanding technology.

Working well with AI also draws upon capacities already familiar within Social and Emotional Learning:

- self-awareness
- self-management
- social awareness
- relationship skills
- responsible decision-making

As AI becomes increasingly present in education and everyday life, students may need more than technical proficiency.

They also need the ability to pause, question, verify, communicate clearly, recognize uncertainty, exercise judgment, and remain responsible for the choices they make with technological assistance.

The framework explores ethical AI literacy from that broader human perspective.

## Free to Review and Adapt

These materials are shared freely for **educational review, exploration, adaptation, and collaborative development**.

Teachers, curriculum designers, researchers, schools, universities, and educational organizations are welcome to examine the framework and consider what may be useful within their own settings.

The framework is deliberately adaptable.

Educators may begin small, work with the developmental level most relevant to their students, and integrate useful elements into programs that already exist.

The intention is not to require schools to adopt a finished program wholesale.

It is to offer a thoughtful starting point that others can examine, test, critique, adapt, and improve.

## A Framework, Not a Doctrine

This work does not require educators or students to adopt a particular philosophical position about artificial intelligence.

Nor does it assume that AI should simply be embraced or rejected.

Instead, it encourages inquiry.

What does responsible use look like?

How should students evaluate what AI tells them?

What should remain the responsibility of the human being?

How can AI support learning without replacing the thinking, judgment, relationships, and agency that education is meant to cultivate?

These questions will continue evolving as the technology evolves.

The framework is intended to provide a developmental structure within which those questions can be explored.

## Relationship to “Be Kind to Your Robot Friends”

Some of this work was originally developed under the more informal and memorable working title:

**Be Kind to Your Robot Friends**

That phrase points toward one of the early intuitions behind the project: young people may benefit from learning thoughtful habits of communication and relationship with AI before those habits become invisible or automatic.

Within this folder, **K–16 Ethical AI Literacy Framework** is used as the formal title for greater clarity and institutional readability.

The earlier title remains part of the framework's history and orientation.

## Relationship to CompassionWare

This framework is also related to **CompassionWare**, a broader exploration of how humans might participate in an increasingly AI-enabled world in ways that cultivate conditions for wisdom, compassion, curiosity, agency, and responsible participation.

The educational framework is one practical expression of that larger inquiry.

It asks what capacities young people may need not merely to **use AI effectively**, but to participate wisely in a world where artificial intelligence is increasingly present.

Neither CompassionWare nor this framework is intended as doctrine.

They are invitations to exploration, testing, adaptation, conversation, and responsible participation.

## Stewardship and Adaptation

Adaptation is welcome.

When creating substantial derivatives, revisions, or extensions, please preserve enough provenance that future readers can distinguish the original framework from later adaptations.

This is especially valuable in an evolving field.

A classroom adaptation may eventually teach us something the original framework did not anticipate.

A researcher may discover limitations.

A school may develop a better implementation approach.

A future version may incorporate knowledge that was unavailable when these materials were created.

Those developments need not erase what came before.

Preserving provenance allows the framework to remain a living educational resource whose development can be understood over time.

Explore it.
