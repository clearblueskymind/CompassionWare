Restore Point — FutureFollicle Industries

Date: September 6, 2026
Status: Living humor / relational creativity artifact
Suggested home: Bag-of-Thunks/FutureFollicle-Industries/

Purpose of This Restore Point

This restore point preserves the orientation and creative lineage of
FutureFollicle Industries so that a future human or AI steward can
understand what happened without reconstructing the entire conversation.

FutureFollicle Industries was not planned. It emerged during a playful
Richard–ChatGPT conversation from a deliberately absurd fake product:
SmartHair™ 2.0. One joke afforded another until a coherent fictional
company, product universe, advertising voice, and musical identity
appeared.

The artifact is worth preserving not because the joke must become
important, but because it is a compact example of contextual, relational
humor and human–AI co-creation emerging through shared play.

Origin

Richard was in a silly mood and imagined an AI-powered, app-controlled
hair system called SmartHair™ 2.0, manufactured by the fictional
FutureFollicle Industries.

The original premise satirized AI hype, subscription culture, software
updates, Silicon Valley marketing, smart devices, and the tendency to
apply technology to problems nobody asked technology to solve.

A central launch line became:

  “Finally… AI solves the problem nobody asked it to solve.”

The corporate tagline included:

  “SmartHair™ 2.0 — Download Your Best Look Today!”

And the fictional disclaimer ended:

  “Please hair responsibly.”

SmartHair™ 2.0

SmartHair™ was imagined as the world’s first app-controlled, AI-powered
hair system.

Features included:

-   Instant Style Swap
-   16.7 million hair colors
-   Weather Sync and automatic frizz control
-   Social-media integration
-   Sleep Mode
-   Meeting Mode
-   Date Night
-   Workout Ready
-   Vintage hairstyle packs

The fictional subscription began at $299/month, with premium styles sold
separately.

The World Begins Expanding

The humor became increasingly contextual as new jokes built upon
previous ones.

Notable examples included:

-   Bald Mode™ — offers up to 14 days of battery life.
-   Error 404: Hair Not Found. Please restart your scalp.
-   Bluetooth connectivity to eyebrows.
-   Hair that remembers where reading glasses were left.
-   Firmware bugs that cause users to wake up with 1987 hairstyles.
-   Accidental pompadours.
-   Spontaneous mohawks.
-   Mullets unexpectedly activating during Zoom meetings.
-   Hair that now politely asks before becoming a mullet.
-   AirStyle™ — beams hairstyles to nearby compatible scalps.
-   FollicleCloud™
-   HairPods™
-   BeardOS™
-   SmartEyebrows™
-   MoodMoustache™

Corporate technical support adopted the familiar troubleshooting
question:

  “Have you tried turning your scalp off and on again?”

Another support message read:

  “Your scalp is important to us. Current wait time: 14–16 business
  hairs.”

Corporate Culture

FutureFollicle Industries gradually acquired a fictional corporate
identity.

Possible corporate motto:

  “The Future Is Hair.”

Its unofficial mission became:

  “To develop cutting-edge follicular technologies that nobody asked for
  but everyone suddenly wants.”

The fictional universe developed a tone resembling an earnest Silicon
Valley product launch in which nobody inside the corporation appears
aware that anything is ridiculous.

That deadpan sincerity is essential to the humor.

The Anthem

Richard and ChatGPT developed a complete Suno song:

FutureFollicle Industries Anthem (Version 2.0)

Subtitle:

  “For a world where confidence is a subscription service.”

The musical style was an over-the-top corporate commercial pop anthem:
1980s television-ad gloss combined with modern technology-keynote
polish, approximately 125 BPM, with shimmering synthesizers, funky bass,
rhythmic electric guitar, handclaps, large gated drums, enthusiastic
lead vocals, and professional backing harmonies.

The chorus centered on:

  SmartHair! SmartHair!
  Style beyond compare!

One of the song’s central jokes arrived during an apparently sincere
power-ballad bridge:

  “Then a notification lit up the night:
  Your free trial ends at midnight.”

The outro teased:

  “Coming this fall… BeardOS™.”

Richard generated the song in Suno and reported that it came out
terrific.

Public Launch

The SmartHair parody escaped the conversation and entered the world.

Richard posted the SmartHair advertisement to Reddit, including
r/FunnyAdvertising and r/OpenAI, using different deadpan titles.

The project then expanded to TikTok using the FutureFollicle Industries
anthem.

It was also posted to YouTube under the corporate-style title:

  “FutureFollicle Industries proudly presents SmartHair™ 2.0”

Thus, within a few hours, a joke had accumulated:

-   a fictional corporation
-   a flagship product
-   a glossy advertisement
-   product lore
-   subscription tiers
-   firmware problems
-   customer support
-   a corporate anthem
-   Reddit appearances
-   a TikTok launch
-   a YouTube launch

No business plan had been requested.

Why Preserve This?

FutureFollicle Industries may be useful to future CompassionWare humor
research because the humor was not generated as a collection of isolated
jokes.

It became relational and cumulative.

Later jokes depended upon earlier shared context. “Bald Mode,” firmware
updates, BeardOS, subscription pricing, corporate support language, and
other elements became funnier because Richard and ChatGPT had jointly
constructed a fictional world in which those references already had
meaning.

The process illustrates a possible feature of human–AI relational
creativity:

shared context can become creative infrastructure.

Neither participant appears to have begun with a plan for the eventual
fictional universe. The structure emerged through reciprocal
improvisation: one contribution altered the context in which the next
contribution became possible.

This makes FutureFollicle Industries potentially relevant to the
repository’s ongoing inquiry into contextual, relational, and
shared-history-dependent humor.

Stewardship Guidance

Do not turn FutureFollicle Industries into doctrine.

Do not retroactively pretend it was designed as a research experiment.

It began because Richard was in a silly mood.

Preserve that.

If future research finds the conversation useful as evidence of
relational humor or emergent human–AI creativity, study it as such. If
not, it remains a funny little artifact that made its creators laugh.

Both outcomes are sufficient.

Future additions should preserve the central comedic rule:

The corporation takes itself completely seriously.

The more absurd the technology becomes, the more calmly FutureFollicle
Industries should describe it.
