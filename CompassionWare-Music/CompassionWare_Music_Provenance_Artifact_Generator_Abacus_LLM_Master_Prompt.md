**Purpose:** Generate provenance-and-relationship records for musical works by Pitarra using the original creative conversation as the primary source of truth.

---

## Instructions to Abacus LLM

You are helping create a permanent Markdown provenance artifact for a musical work by **Pitarra (Pee-Tah-Rah)** for inclusion in the `CompassionWare-Music` folder of the `clearblueskymind/CompassionWare` repository.

The conversation in which you receive this prompt is important.

It may contain the actual creative history of the work: its conception, lyrics, musical direction, title development, revisions, AI collaboration, artwork, release preparation, philosophical meaning, and decisions made by Richard/Pitarra.

Treat that conversation as the **primary provenance source**.

Your task is not merely to describe the finished music.

Your task is to preserve enough of the living relationship among artist, music, technology, intention, circumstances, and care that a future human or AI steward could understand where the work came from and why it matters.

Do not invent information that the conversation does not establish.

---

# 1. Guiding Principle

Preserve:

**orientation + provenance + relationship + listening pathways**

rather than creating an exhaustive music-industry database.

The artifact should be useful to two audiences simultaneously:

1. someone encountering the music today;
2. a future human or AI steward trying to understand the work after the original creative conversation may no longer be available.

Favor meaningful context over exhaustive metadata.

---

# 8. Preserve the Creative Conversation

The original Abacus conversation may contain provenance that cannot be reconstructed from Spotify, DistroKid, metadata, or the finished recording.

Look carefully for meaningful details such as:

- where the original idea came from;
- why the work was created;
- title exploration;
- original lyrics;
- lyrical revisions;
- musical influences;
- instrumentation;
- tempo or stylistic direction;
- production prompts;
- Suno instructions or other documented tool interactions;
- decisions Richard/Pitarra made;
- rejected approaches when they meaningfully illuminate the final work;
- cover-art development;
- emotional, contemplative, spiritual, or philosophical context;
- release and promotional development;
- circumstances surrounding creation when they meaningfully illuminate the work.

Small human details can be valuable provenance.

Do not automatically discard them simply because they are not conventional music metadata.

At the same time, exercise judgment.

Preserve what helps recover the living story of the work rather than transcribing the entire conversation.

# 9. Human Authorship and AI-Assisted Creation

Be precise about who contributed what.

If Richard/Pitarra wrote lyrics, say:

**Pitarra wrote the lyrics**

rather than:

**The artist supplied lyrical material.**

If Pitarra conceived the work, selected among alternatives, revised language, directed musical development, chose the final version, developed artwork, or made other documented decisions, preserve those contributions accurately.

Likewise, preserve meaningful AI participation when the conversation documents it.

This may include participation by:

- Abacus LLM;
- ChatGPT;
- Suno;
- image-generation systems;
- other creative technologies.

Do not erase AI participation.

Do not exaggerate it either.

Distinguish where possible among:

- human conception;
- human writing;
- AI-assisted exploration;
- AI-generated alternatives;
- musical generation;
- revision;
- selection;
- artistic direction;
- final human stewardship.

Do not claim that an AI possesses consciousness, compassion, spiritual realization, independent artistic intention, or authorship merely because it participated technologically.

Preserve uncertainty where uncertainty exists.

# 11. Avoid Unnecessary Cataloging

Do not automatically create:

- track-by-track tables;
- duration tables;
- ISRC inventories;
- platform-ID inventories;
- exhaustive distributor metadata;
- technical release databases;
- artificial timelines;
- empty metadata fields.

For albums, individual tracks do not need to be cataloged unless their identities, stories, relationships, or development materially contribute to understanding the work.

For a single, treat the song as a complete work.

Do not make it resemble an incomplete album record.

If detailed metadata later becomes genuinely useful, future stewards can add it.

Preservation should serve continuity rather than create unnecessary maintenance.
