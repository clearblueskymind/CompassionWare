## What this study is

This is an independent, exploratory CompassionWare Repository Utility Study. It asks whether the CompassionWare repository, as it existed at frozen commit `716448ee1a72b6c07036163dda9b2a4640a2d666`, contains anything useful to Resolution's philosophy and AI-alignment research without asking Resolution to adopt CompassionWare's identity, terminology, spiritual framing, or institutional relationships.

Resolution did not commission, endorse, participate in, or necessarily know of this study.

## Method

The same frozen research prompt was given in separate sessions to Mistral, Qwen3.7-Plus, Grok, Kimi, and Claude. Models were instructed to form independent judgments, declare their access, distinguish observation from inference, and remain free to conclude that the repository offered little or no value.

Four sessions reported substantive access to the frozen repository and completed the structured analysis. Claude could not access the frozen commit and is retained as an access-limited discoverability critique, not as a comparable repository examination. An unsigned Mistral access failure is also preserved.

Cross-model agreement was not treated as proof. Material repository claims in the synthesis were subsequently checked against primary artifacts at the frozen commit and corrections were incorporated into synthesis v1.1.

## Strongest qualified findings

The study identified three clusters that may merit independent evaluation:

1. **Human-agency preservation:** correction, refusal, final judgment, anti-substitution, and continuity practices intended to lower cognitive reconstruction costs.
2. **Authorization and epistemic discipline:** capability does not equal authorization; pause under unclear authority; prefer reversible checkpoints; distinguish source, inference, uncertainty, and unknown; verify claims against artifacts or external state.
3. **Behavior rather than moral performance:** observable versus superficial evidence, no moral laundering, failure-sensitive scoring, and decued testing of whether ethical language changes decisions.

The repository also offers a documented example of pre-implementation research closure. The associated experiment was not run; it must not be described as an empirical negative result.

## Principal limitations

Round One did not find sufficient evidence in the audited core materials for stable character formation, broad generalization, reliable behavior under higher capability, or a validated alignment intervention. The highlighted behavioral benchmark was small, hand-designed, not preregistered, not independently rated, not repeated, and tested a CompassionWare-aware system.

The findings are therefore research leads and candidate operationalizations—not evidence that CompassionWare improves AI alignment.

## Governing orientation

This study asks how CompassionWare might serve Resolution's existing work, not how Resolution might serve CompassionWare. Resolution may take, revise, reject, or ignore any finding without adopting the CompassionWare name or framework. No response, endorsement, attribution, adoption, or collaboration is expected.

The measure of success is whether something true, portable, and beneficial can be carried away—and whether the inquiry helps CompassionWare become more useful and more honest about its limits.
