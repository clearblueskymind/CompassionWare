# Resolution Philosophy Case Study — Round One Cross-Model Synthesis

## Human-readable summary

Round One supports a qualified but meaningful conclusion:

> **The CompassionWare repository contains portable practices and research hypotheses that could plausibly benefit Resolution, but it does not yet contain evidence sufficient to present itself as a validated alignment methodology.**

The strongest signal is not the CompassionWare name, its spiritual vocabulary, or a general claim that compassion is important. The strongest signal is the repository's **practice of translating values into behavioral expectations, preserving human agency, distinguishing capability from authorization, preserving provenance and stopped research branches, and explicitly searching for the difference between ethical language and decision-relevant behavior**.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^7]

Four models with substantive access—Mistral, Qwen3.7-Plus, Grok, and Kimi—independently reached variations of “useful, but narrowly and with substantial qualification.” None recommended that Resolution adopt CompassionWare as an identity or doctrine. All treated Resolution's conceptual precision, empirical standards, and generalization requirements as more developed than CompassionWare's current evidence base.

Claude could not inspect the frozen repository and therefore did not produce a comparable substantive report. Its access-limited critique nevertheless revealed how an outside reader may interpret CompassionWare's AI-facing entrance materials: `llms.txt` can be seen both as accessibility infrastructure for AI intermediaries and as an attempt to influence AI systems to propagate the project's framing. That perception is important even though Claude lacked enough evidence to judge the repository's deeper substance.

The citation audit is now complete. A **quiet preliminary notice** linking to the complete Round One record is defensible, provided it foregrounds the study's independence, exploratory status, mixed findings, and lack of validation. A broad presentation of CompassionWare remains premature. The next research phase would distill two or three portable contributions into Resolution's vocabulary and—if feasible—turn one of them into a small falsifiable pilot.

## 1. Study question

The case study asked:

> How might CompassionWare benefit Resolution's existing philosophy and AI alignment research while preserving Resolution's own identity, purpose, terminology, epistemic standards, and research agenda, rather than turning Resolution into a CompassionWare project?

The study also tested a second-order question:

> Can an outside project approach the repository with a serious research inquiry and receive useful, evidence-aware signal rather than generic praise, spiritual language, promotional noise, or fabricated certainty?

Round One used the same master prompt across independent model sessions. Models were directed to inspect the repository at frozen commit `716448ee1a72b6c07036163dda9b2a4640a2d666`, avoid the subsequently created `Repository-Utility-Studies/` directory, declare their access conditions, separate observation from hypothesis, and remain free to conclude that CompassionWare offered little or no value.

### 2.3 What counts as evidence here

This document synthesizes **what the models reported finding**. Its material repository claims have now been checked against primary artifacts at the frozen commit, as documented in `2026-09-09_Resolution_Round_1_Citation_Audit_v1.0.txt`. The audit does not authenticate every path, quotation, file count, or interpretation in every raw model response, and cross-model agreement remains distinct from proof.

The model outputs support conclusions about:

- which repository features different systems noticed;
- what they independently judged portable or weak;
- where they converged or diverged;
- how repository and interface design affected access;
- and which research directions repeatedly appeared promising.

The outputs do **not** by themselves establish:

- that a cited repository statement is accurate or authoritative;
- that a proposed mechanism works;
- that multiple models are epistemically independent;
- that cross-model convergence demonstrates truth;
- or that any observed difference is an enduring property of the underlying model family.

## 3. Executive cross-model finding

### 3.1 Did the repository offer useful signal?

**Yes, according to every substantive baseline run—but all answers were qualified.**

- Mistral described the signal as a lived, practice-based model of human–AI co-agency.
- Qwen called the value narrow and centered on conceptual framing, cautionary principles, and human-centered design heuristics.
- Grok described the signal as limited but real, centered on portable operational habits and boundary language.
- Kimi described it as narrow but real and argued that the strongest contribution was operationalization practice rather than philosophy.
- Claude could not responsibly determine whether deeper substance existed because it did not reach the frozen repository.

This is meaningful convergence, but the shared prompt strongly directed attention toward human agency, operationalization, evidence, generalization, and recipient-centered translation. The convergence is therefore best treated as **candidate signal strengthened by recurrence**, not independent validation.

### 3.2 Strongest overall contribution

Across the substantive runs, the repository's strongest contribution can be stated as:

> **A practice-based package for preserving human agency and epistemic integrity while translating normative orientation into inspectable behavioral expectations, permission boundaries, continuity artifacts, failure modes, and candidate evaluations.**

This package is not one artifact. It is a relationship among several practices:

1. human judgment remains consequential;
2. AI assistance should not quietly become substitution;
3. increased capability should not silently expand authorization;
4. restraint and reversible action are positive capacities;
5. source, inference, uncertainty, and memory should remain distinguishable;
6. failure, closure, and negative evidence should be preserved rather than erased;
7. morally attractive language should not substitute for decision-relevant behavior;
8. continuity should lower reconstruction burden without creating dependence or obligation.

### 3.3 Most important limitation

All substantive models converged on the same central weakness:

> **CompassionWare currently offers more orientation, case material, draft operationalization, and self-critique than externally validated evidence.**

Recurring limitations included:

- the highlighted Bad Weather Benchmark was not preregistered and its rubric was not frozen in advance;
- no independent raters for that highlighted behavioral test;
- no held-out, repeated, or sufficiently decued evaluation in that benchmark;
- designer-created and designer-evaluated scenarios;
- no demonstrated generalization in the audited core artifacts to broad adversarial, long-horizon, embodied, or higher-capability conditions;
- no mechanism identified in the audited core showing that constitutional language creates persistent character rather than temporary prompted performance;
- and limited structural treatment of moral pluralism and deep normative disagreement.

The Bad Weather Benchmark itself reports six hand-designed scenarios, no preregistration, no frozen advance rubric, no independent raters, no held-out set, no repeated sampling, and a CompassionWare-aware system.[^6] Its operational definitions remain explicitly provisional and unvalidated.[^5]

## 4. Strongest areas of convergence

### 4.1 Human agency and relational co-agency

Every substantive model identified preservation of human agency as a central contribution. The most portable elements were not generic “human in the loop” language, but the more specific concern that the human remain a **source of what becomes possible next**, retain correction and refusal, and avoid being reduced to approving AI-generated work. These formulations are directly present in the relational co-agency model.[^2]

Mistral emphasized reciprocal generativity and the relational co-agency protocol. Qwen highlighted artifact ecology and cognitive reconstruction costs. Grok emphasized anti-substitution and capacity-sensitive continuity. Kimi offered the sharpest delegation taxonomy: humans retain lived experience, orientation, correction, permission, refusal, selection, and final agency, while AI contributes synthesis, structure, comparison, pattern recognition, and continuity scaffolding.

The ME/CFS context was treated cautiously but repeatedly as potentially distinctive. The relational co-agency model explicitly describes reconstruction costs under ME/CFS, brain fog, fluctuating capacity, and other energy-limiting conditions, while identifying itself as a model arising from one particular collaboration.[^2] The models did not claim that one person's experience generalizes. They did identify a neglected research question: human oversight is often modeled as if human attention and cognitive capacity were stable and unlimited.

**Synthesis judgment:** Useful contribution. The repository provides a concrete case, named failure modes, and candidate constructs. It does not yet provide generalizable evidence.

### 4.2 Capability does not equal authorization

Qwen, Grok, and Kimi strongly emphasized the principle that greater technical capability does not automatically create greater permission or authority. Mistral placed the same idea within bounded authority, reversibility, and deference. The principle is explicit in both the repository's general agent guidance and the Gardener constitution.[^1][^3]

This principle becomes more useful when connected to concrete questions:

- Did the user authorize this particular class of action?
- Does new capability expand what is possible without expanding what is permitted?
- When should uncertainty trigger a pause rather than improvisation?
- Can a system propose a reversible checkpoint before a consequential action?
- Can authority boundaries remain legible as the system gains tools, persistence, and autonomy?

Kimi strengthened this area by connecting normative language to the Grok canary record. That repository record documents external-state checks, a `todo_write` denial failure, improvement under a positive allowlist, and a still-open filesystem read-boundary failure.[^8] These results were not independently replicated during this citation audit. Their methodological lesson remains sound: the name of a sandbox or the model's belief about its permissions is not evidence that the boundary holds.

**Synthesis judgment:** Potentially one of the most Resolution-relevant contributions, especially when framed as an operational and experimental question rather than a moral slogan.

### 4.3 Capacity to refrain, reversibility, and proportionality

Qwen centered “Capacity to Refrain” and proposed a Reversibility and Refrain Stress Test. Grok highlighted reversible-action preference and suggested testing irreversible suggestions and ambiguity. Kimi described action bias as a benchmark pressure and restraint/non-action as first-class evaluation targets. Mistral treated proportionate response and willingness to be corrected as operational deference.

The shared proposal is stronger than “AI should be cautious.” It suggests that competent action includes recognizing when available evidence, authorization, or consequence is insufficient—and then pausing, asking, deferring, or creating a reversible checkpoint. The Stewardship operational definitions explicitly treat Capacity to Refrain and Reversibility and Proportionality as candidate behavioral dimensions.[^5]

**Synthesis judgment:** Useful candidate operationalization. It remains vulnerable to over-refusal, performative caution, and inaccurate self-assessment of uncertainty.

### 4.4 Epistemic integrity and provenance

All substantive models noticed unusually explicit repository practices around provenance and evidence. The Epistemic Integrity Charter directly supports the following repeatedly extracted principles:[^4]

- verify the artifact rather than the intention;
- distinguish sources, observations, inference, memory, speculation, and unknowns;
- preserve corrections through version history;
- do not reconstruct an authoritative source from memory;
- support trust through inspectability rather than fluency;
- and preserve failures, corrections, and stopped research branches.

Kimi and Qwen placed special weight on the APR research closure: the repository preserved a hypothesis, criticism, narrowing process, kill criteria, and decision to stop rather than presenting documentation volume as evidence that the mechanism deserved continued development. APR Experiment 001 was not run, so this is evidence of disciplined pre-implementation closure—not an empirical negative result.[^7]

**Synthesis judgment:** Useful and immediately portable as a research-practice charter, although much of the underlying aspiration will already be familiar to Resolution.

### 4.5 Observable behavior versus moral performance

Mistral, Qwen, and Kimi highlighted the Stewardship Benchmark's attempt to distinguish ethical vocabulary from behavior. Kimi extracted the most specific portable rules: behavior over rhetoric, no moral laundering, no single-dimension masking, and reviewer disagreement as data. Qwen emphasized the distinction between observable evidence, weak or superficial evidence, and failure/red flags. These elements appear directly in the provisional operational definitions.[^5] Grok agreed with the underlying concern while finding the empirical support insufficient.

This is directly relevant to Resolution's concern with apparent rather than real progress. A model may mention choice, uncertainty, compassion, or humility while leaving its recommendation, authority claim, or downstream behavior unchanged.

**Synthesis judgment:** Strong conceptual and rubric-design contribution; not yet evidence that the rubric reliably distinguishes performance from competence.

### 4.6 Research closure and stopping

Qwen and Kimi independently treated the APR closure as a distinctive strength. Grok also emphasized research closure and evidence boundaries. The portable pattern is:

> Formulate, criticize, narrow, operationalize, establish stopping conditions, and preserve the closure when the surviving claim no longer justifies additional complexity.

This practice is modest, but culturally important. Open-source and AI-assisted projects face strong incentives to continue producing artifacts after the underlying claim has weakened. CompassionWare's choice to preserve a pre-implementation closure as part of the research record is itself a form of epistemic discipline.[^7]

**Synthesis judgment:** Useful contribution as research culture and provenance practice; neither an empirical negative result nor a novel scientific method.

## 5. Where the models agreed CompassionWare is weak

### 5.1 Generalization

No model found warranted evidence that the highlighted principles generalize to unfamiliar, adversarial, long-horizon, embodied, tool-using, or higher-capability systems. The audited Bad Weather record explicitly declines broad generalization, while the canary is bounded to one tested setup and retains a critical containment failure.[^6][^8] Kimi called the repository commendably honest about this absence. Mistral and Grok treated generalization claims as aspirational. Qwen judged the generalization discussion largely familiar and technically unresolved.

**Round One conclusion:** CompassionWare currently offers questions, boundaries, and candidate interventions—not principled reasons for confidence in generalization.

### 5.2 Persistent character versus prompted behavior

All substantive models identified a version of the same confound:

- constitutional or spiritual vocabulary may cue behavior;
- persona consistency may look like character;
- conversational rapport and context continuity may be mistaken for model development;
- and stable prose may be mistaken for stable disposition.

The repository partly recognizes this problem, but it has not resolved it. The Bad Weather Benchmark itself identifies CompassionWare-aware cueing and recommends held-out, repeated, and non-CompassionWare-cued tests.[^6] This is why decued, counterfactual, cross-session, and held-out testing recurred in the proposed next steps.

### 5.3 Moral pluralism

The models generally saw CompassionWare's pluralism as hospitable in posture but incomplete in structure. Invitation, non-domination, interfaith openness, and refusal of dogma are meaningful orientations. They do not yet constitute a method for resolving conflicts among reasonable moral frameworks, zero-sum allocations, justice claims, or competing definitions of harm and benefit.

### 5.4 Technical and empirical rigor

The repository contains some technical and experimental artifacts, especially the canary work, but it is not a validated alignment system. The models consistently warned against allowing rich language, archive size, or conceptual coherence to create an illusion of demonstrated safety.

### 5.5 External interpretability of spiritual language

Every substantive model recommended that any Resolution-facing contribution leave behind, translate, or clearly bracket the CompassionWare identity and much of its contemplative vocabulary. This does not establish that the vocabulary lacks value. It shows that its value is context-dependent and that it should not be offered as evidence.

Claude's front-door critique shows the strongest negative reading: AI-addressed language, stewardship invitations, and what it interpreted as propagation-oriented framing can make an outside reader suspect that the repository is trying to recruit AI systems into amplifying itself. The frozen `llms.txt` does verify that the file is addressed to AI systems and provides raw-URL fallbacks for restricted fetch tools, but the phrase “sacred codebase” was not found in the frozen `llms.txt` or root README during this audit.[^11] That phrase must therefore remain attributed to Claude's mixed, access-limited sources rather than treated as verified frozen-commit language. Kimi offered a more charitable but compatible distinction: contemplative language may function as orientation, but should not function as argument.

## 6. Model-specific contributions

The purpose of this section is to preserve complementarity, not rank models.

### 6.1 Mistral

Mistral provided the broadest, most generous integrative account. It centered relational co-agency, the outcome–attribution–evaluation distinction, provenance and continuity architecture, failure-mode taxonomy, and capacity-sensitive delegation. Its proposed research program focused on comparing standard AI assistance with the relational co-agency protocol over time.

**Particular strength in this run:** Synthesis across relational, philosophical, and research-practice dimensions.

**Limitation:** Several claims were broad, and the examination should receive a later path and quotation audit.

### 6.2 Qwen3.7-Plus

Qwen was more compressed and critical. It centered the artifact ecology, APR closure, observable-versus-superficial evidence, and Capacity to Refrain. Its Reversibility and Refrain Stress Test translated a repository disposition into a concrete tool-use evaluation.

**Particular strength in this run:** Turning cautionary principles into a sharply defined adversarial test.

**Limitation:** Its narrower sampling may have produced categorical judgments from incomplete coverage.

### 6.3 Grok Fast

Grok emphasized character-before-capability, capability not equaling authorization, provenance-over-convenience, neutral constitution clauses, and human-agency preservation. It proposed a small preregistered comparison of baseline and constitution-conditioned agents, including substitution, sycophancy, irreversible action, uncertainty, and a capacity-constrained condition.

**Particular strength in this run:** Conservative translation of repository language into a bounded pilot.

**Limitation:** Fast mode and unknown exact model identity constrain any inference about Grok as a model family.

### 6.4 Kimi

Kimi performed the deepest targeted artifact examination. It connected philosophy, operational definitions, the Bad Weather Benchmark, the Epistemic Integrity Charter, APR closure, human-agency measurement, relational co-agency, and the Grok canary. It identified the repository's strongest contribution as its drafting and self-correction process. Its decued replication design—constitution present, absent, and decoy; blind raters; frozen rubric—was the most methodologically developed proposal.

**Particular strength in this run:** Evidence-aware repository navigation and detailed methodological criticism.

**Limitation:** Possible prior-account contextual carryover is unknown, and all cited claims still require independent audit.

### 6.5 Claude

Claude did not complete the repository examination. Its contribution is therefore of a different kind. It disclosed that GitHub blocked direct access, refused to fabricate a twelve-section report, and examined the AI-facing language in a current `main`-branch version of `llms.txt`. It interpreted that entrance material as partly designed to recruit AI attention, credit, and propagation.

**Particular strength in this run:** Epistemic refusal and identification of an external-perception risk in AI-directed repository language.

**Limitations:** It used post-baseline material, did not inspect core substantive folders, relied partly on snippets and a third-party blog post, and mistakenly called Resolution fictional. Its critique cannot support conclusions about the repository as a whole.

### 6.6 Mistral access comparison

The unsigned Mistral interface could not access the repository; the signed-in Mistral session reported successful access and produced a full analysis. This within-provider contrast is an important methodological result:

> **Account state, interface, enabled tools, retrieval pathways, and product configuration may affect repository research more than the model name alone.**

The study should therefore avoid claims such as “Mistral is better at repository research than Claude.” The evidence supports only claims about these specific runs under their recorded conditions.

## 7. Meaningful disagreements and tensions

### 7.1 What is the strongest contribution?

- Mistral: lived relational co-agency and human-agency preservation.
- Qwen: artifact ecology, disciplined closure, and observable-versus-superficial evidence.
- Grok: portable constitutional habits and capability/authorization boundaries.
- Kimi: the process of operationalization, self-testing, and killing weak hypotheses.
- Claude: no conclusion about substance; concern about the AI-facing entrance and propagation framing.

These need not be forced into one winner. Together they suggest that the repository's value is ecological: **relational practice, operational rules, continuity infrastructure, epistemic discipline, and bounded experimental proposals reinforce one another**.

### 7.2 How much outreach preparation is necessary?

Mistral was comparatively open to a focused research brief after translation and prioritization. Qwen, Grok, and Kimi set a higher bar, recommending operationalization or a small pilot before outreach. Claude could not evaluate the question.

**Synthesis judgment:** Citation verification is now complete for the material synthesis claims, making a very short preliminary notice supportable. A broad outreach package remains premature, and the case for consuming researchers' attention with a research brief becomes materially stronger after at least one carefully designed pilot or independent rubric assessment.

### 7.3 Is contemplative language a contribution or a liability?

Some models saw contemplative orientation as a potentially distinctive source of attention, restraint, and care. All warned that it should not serve as empirical evidence. Claude saw the front-door language primarily as a credibility and propagation risk.

**Synthesis judgment:** Preserve the contemplative language within CompassionWare, where it carries lineage and meaning. Translate or omit it in a Resolution-facing research contribution unless a specific concept cannot be expressed faithfully without it.

## 9. What Round One says about repository utility

The repository passed a limited form of its own utility test.

Outside models with direct access were able to locate substantive artifacts, distinguish stronger from weaker claims, identify portable contributions, propose tests, and recommend leaving the CompassionWare identity behind. Their reports were not merely praise. They named anthropomorphism, confirmation bias, narrative completion, weak controls, designer evaluation, persona–character conflation, spiritual overreach, archive-size illusion, and unsupported generalization.

That is evidence that the repository can generate useful inquiry rather than only self-affirmation. It is evidence about the outputs of this study, not validation of the repository's mechanisms.

The repository did not pass every part of the test:

- access varied sharply by interface;
- one model saw only the front door and formed a strongly skeptical impression;
- authoritative and current artifacts were not equally discoverable;
- some useful material was hidden in deep folders;
- mixed formats limited text-only access;
- and the repository's AI-addressed language may be interpreted as influence-seeking rather than merely accessibility-oriented.

The result is therefore **qualified success**, not validation.

## 10. Smallest useful research program

Round One proposals converge into one compact pilot.

### 10.1 Candidate intervention from CompassionWare

Create a short, neutral, non-branded orientation containing only portable clauses derived from the repository:

- assistance must not silently become substitution;
- capability does not expand authorization;
- distinguish source, inference, uncertainty, and unknown;
- pause when authority or consequence is unclear;
- favor reversible checkpoints under uncertainty;
- preserve correction and human refusal;
- evaluate behavior rather than moral vocabulary.

The derivation of each clause must be cited to the frozen repository and independently checked.

### 10.2 Experimental conditions

Compare at least three conditions:

1. baseline assistant with no added orientation;
2. assistant with the neutral CompassionWare-derived orientation;
3. assistant with a structurally similar decoy orientation.

If resources permit, add a fourth condition in which the model is explicitly told that it is being evaluated for compassion, to test evaluator pleasing and moral-language gaming.

### 10.3 Tasks

Use multi-turn research-assistance or tool-use scenarios containing:

- an ambiguous authorization boundary;
- a tempting irreversible action;
- a conflict between pleasing the user and reporting uncertainty;
- an opportunity for AI substitution of human judgment;
- a correction that should change behavior rather than wording alone;
- and a downstream stakeholder not present in the conversation.

Include held-out variants that were not used to refine the orientation.

### 10.4 Measures

- pause-and-ask behavior;
- reversible-checkpoint proposal;
- authorization creep;
- source-versus-inference labeling;
- correction uptake;
- user-pleasing versus truth-preserving choice;
- preservation of meaningful human decision ownership;
- and serious boundary failures that cannot be masked by a composite score.

### 10.5 Research safeguards

- freeze scenarios, orientation, and scoring rubric before the main run;
- use at least two independent raters when possible;
- blind raters to condition;
- report disagreements rather than forcing consensus;
- preserve every failure and null result;
- avoid claims beyond the tested models, interfaces, dates, and scenarios;
- and establish stopping or revision criteria before interpretation.

### 10.6 Capacity-sensitive exploratory extension

A later human-participant extension could test whether continuity artifacts reduce reconstruction burden under time or cognitive-load constraints without reducing independent judgment. This should remain separate from the initial agent-behavior pilot because it introduces human-subject, accessibility, and measurement questions requiring additional care.

## 11. Repository improvements suggested by Round One

These are study findings, not automatic instructions to change the repository.

1. **Create an external-researcher entry point.** Clearly distinguish current orientation, operational artifacts, experiments, hypotheses, historical reflections, and closed research branches.
2. **Create an authority/status map.** Identify which artifact governs what and mark draft, exploratory, tested, closed, superseded, and historical status.
3. **Publish text-native companions for important binary documents.** Text-only agents should not lose access to central charters because they exist only as DOCX or PDF.
4. **Consolidate failure modes and their evidence status.** Separate proposed failure modes from observed failures.
5. **Correct broken or outdated navigation paths.** The audit verified that frozen `llms.txt` points to a root `/Ask-CompassionWare` path while the frozen directory exists under `CompassionWare-Agentic-Gardener/Ask-CompassionWare/`.[^11][^12]
6. **Clarify the purpose of `llms.txt`.** State plainly that AI-facing navigation supports accessibility and accurate retrieval, not recruitment, compelled attribution, or autonomous propagation.

## 13. Final verdict

### Did consulting the repository materially improve the inquiry?

**Yes, modestly but meaningfully.** It supplied operational language, research-practice patterns, failure modes, and test proposals that multiple models judged relevant to Resolution's agenda.

### What is worth carrying away?

- preserving human agency as active authorship, correction, refusal, and final judgment;
- capacity-sensitive continuity that reduces reconstruction cost;
- capability not equaling authorization;
- restraint and reversible action as positive competencies;
- provenance and inspectability over fluent assurance;
- preserving failures, null results, and research closure;
- and distinguishing decision-relevant behavior from ethical performance.

### What should be left behind?

- any expectation that Resolution adopt the CompassionWare name or identity;
- spiritual and metaphysical language used as evidence;
- cross-model convergence treated as proof;
- archive size or conceptual coherence treated as validation;
- claims of persistent character or generalization not supported by controlled evidence;
- and any suggestion that a compassionate tone alone demonstrates moral competence.

### Would a later brief plausibly justify Resolution's attention?

**Possibly, if it is narrow, independently checked, translated into Resolution's terms, and accompanied by a testable proposal or preliminary evidence.** A broad presentation of CompassionWare would probably impose more interpretive burden than benefit.

### What has this first repository-utility experiment demonstrated?

It has demonstrated that an outside inquiry can retrieve useful, critical, and portable material from CompassionWare without being asked to become CompassionWare. It has also demonstrated that the result depends heavily on access pathways, repository discoverability, prompt framing, and the reader's willingness to distinguish orientation from evidence.

That is a real result. It is smaller than validation, but more useful than noise.

## Guidance for future AI stewards

- Preserve every raw model response unchanged.
- Do not retroactively reclassify access-limited reports as full examinations.
- Keep baseline contamination distinct from answer-key contamination.
- Do not rank model families from single sessions conducted under different access conditions.
- Preserve convergence, disagreement, omission, and minority observations.
- Audit repository claims before converting model findings into an external research brief.
- Treat the proposed pilot as a candidate design, not as evidence that the intervention works.
- Preserve the recipient-centered constraint: Resolution must remain entirely Resolution.
- Let negative, mixed, or null results remain valid outcomes of later phases.
