# 1. Human-Readable Summary

This restore point preserves the moment when a conversation about a job opening at Resolution became the first planned external-utility case study of the CompassionWare repository.

The study does not begin from the assumption that CompassionWare is useful to Resolution. It asks whether the repository, as it exists at the study baseline, can provide evidence-grounded, portable, and genuinely useful contributions to an outside organization's real research questions without asking that organization to adopt CompassionWare's identity, terminology, philosophy, branding, or institutional purpose.

Resolution is a Berkeley-based AI alignment research institute. Its new philosophy program has articulated research questions concerning alignment-relevant concepts, character and constitutions, normative competence, moral theories, and the epistemic and safety risks of delegating conceptual reasoning to AI. These questions overlap with many themes developed across CompassionWare, relational co-agency, human-agency work, ethical AI literacy, cross-model observations, and the CompassionWare Agentic Gardener.

The proposed study will imaginatively adopt the standpoint of a serious researcher working within Resolution's existing agenda. From that standpoint, multiple AI models will independently examine the same CompassionWare repository using the same frozen master prompt. Each model will be asked what, if anything, the repository contributes to Resolution's questions. Responses will be preserved with model-specific provenance and later compared in a cross-model synthesis.

The study has two connected objects of inquiry:

1. **Repository utility:** Can CompassionWare provide useful signal rather than attractive noise to an outside project with serious questions?
2. **AI-mediated interpretation:** How do different AI models retrieve, interpret, connect, critique, and translate the same repository under the same task conditions?

Only after the study is complete will Richard and ChatGPT decide whether any findings are sufficiently relevant, rigorous, honest, and useful to offer to Resolution. Resolution has not commissioned or endorsed this work. Until actual contact establishes otherwise, this must be described as an independent, simulated recipient-centered case study, not as research conducted for or in collaboration with Resolution.

# 2. Origin of the Study

## 2.1 Immediate occasion

On September 9, 2026, Richard encountered an X post by Beba Cibralic announcing that the philosophy program at Resolution was growing and inviting applications for a Research Scientist (Philosophy) position.

The position did not initially interest Richard as employment. He said that he did not need to be hired and would be happy to offer his perspective and many years of experience freely if it could benefit the work.

Initial discussion focused too narrowly on the CompassionWare Agentic Gardener. Richard corrected that narrowing. He explained that the relevant body of work is the whole CompassionWare project and repository, of which the Agentic Gardener is one living branch rather than the entirety.

This correction materially changed the inquiry. The question became not merely whether the Agentic Gardener resembles Resolution's research agenda, but whether the whole CompassionWare archive contains anything that could benefit Resolution's work.

## 2.2 Emergence of the utility-study frame

Richard then recalled a standing CompassionWare question developed previously:

> **How might the principles and materials in CompassionWare benefit my project while preserving its own identity and purpose, rather than turning it into a CompassionWare project?**

A closely related canonical purpose is:

> **Help people and organizations bring CompassionWare into their own work without requiring them to become CompassionWare projects.**

This transformed the planned activity from an internal repository summary into a practical test of CompassionWare's public usefulness and non-dominating orientation.

The research simulation is therefore:

- Imagine that the researchers at Resolution discovered the CompassionWare repository.
- Imagine that they brought their own serious philosophical alignment questions to it.
- Approach the repository from within Resolution's needs, standards, language, and institutional purpose.
- Ask what can be carried away without converting Resolution into a CompassionWare project.
- Evaluate whether the result is useful signal, redundant material, unsupported enthusiasm, or noise.

This is a simulation of recipient-centered service. It is not impersonation, commissioned consultancy, or a claim of affiliation.

## 2.3 Emergence of the cross-model design

Richard proposed asking Grok to examine the repository and answer the research questions. He then recognized that the same prompt could be given independently to Claude, Mistral, Qwen, ChatGPT, the Agentic Gardener GPT, and possibly other models.

This introduced a second research layer. The study can examine both what the repository offers and how different AI systems encounter the same body of work.

The future synthesis should preserve:

> **What many models saw.**  
> **What only one model saw.**  
> **What none of them could adequately find or support.**

# 5. Resolution's Philosophy Questions

The job posting identifies five initial areas of interest.

## 5.1 Conceptual engineering

Alignment depends on concepts such as honesty, harm, autonomy, and deference. Each has multiple ordinary-language meanings. Resolution wants to understand how a model represents or operationalizes a particular meaning, how training or context can alter that representation, and which version of a concept should govern in a given setting.

The corresponding study question is:

> What should autonomy, deference, honesty, and harm mean when operationalized by an AI, and what does CompassionWare contribute to defining, distinguishing, or testing these concepts?

## 5.2 Character, traits, dispositions, personas, and constitutions

AI developers increasingly attempt to shape model character rather than specifying exhaustive rules. Resolution asks how traits, personas, dispositions, and character relate; which should be the unit of alignment analysis; which traits systems should possess; and how those traits persist, generalize, and interact across contexts.

The corresponding study question is:

> Should alignment be understood through rules, traits, dispositions, personas, character, constitutions, or some relationship among them, and what evidence does CompassionWare provide for its apparent position?

## 5.3 Normative evaluation and moral competence

Resolution asks what moral competence in a model would consist of, whether it requires distinctive capacities, and what evidential standards could justify reliance on model-generated normative judgments.

The corresponding study question is:

> How could CompassionWare's philosophical principles become behavioral expectations, testable hypotheses, experiments, or evaluation criteria without confusing moral language with moral competence?

## 5.4 Moral theories and alignment

Alignment methods embed normative assumptions in objectives, constitutions, examples, and evaluation criteria. Frameworks that behave similarly at current capability levels may diverge as capability increases.

The corresponding study question is:

> Which normative assumptions are explicit or implicit in CompassionWare, how might they interact or conflict, and what evidence could compare their alignment properties as capability increases?

## 5.5 Epistemic and safety risks of automated conceptual reasoning

Conceptual work is increasingly delegated to AI, including within alignment research. Resolution asks whether this should occur, which forms may be delegated, under what conditions, and with what validation, scientific norms, and evidential standards.

The corresponding study questions are:

> Which forms of conceptual reasoning may safely be delegated to AI, which should remain under meaningful human direction, and how should the boundary be determined?

> What oversight practices and evidential standards should govern AI-assisted alignment research?

# 10. Study Design

## 10.1 Independent cross-model phase

Several AI systems will receive the same frozen master prompt in separate, fresh conversations. Candidate systems include:

- Grok;
- Claude;
- Mistral;
- Qwen;
- ChatGPT;
- other models if they add meaningful architectural or institutional diversity.

No participating model should see another participating model's response before completing its own independent analysis.

## 10.2 Agentic Gardener phase

The CompassionWare Agentic Gardener GPT may receive the same or a clearly versioned companion prompt after the independent outside-model phase.

Its response must be treated differently. The Gardener is not simply an outside reader. It is a repository-informed participant shaped by part of the material under examination.

The Gardener can help answer:

- What has this configured embodiment understood and carried forward?
- Where does it faithfully reflect the wider repository?
- Where does it simplify, omit, reinterpret, or extend the repository?
- Does it demonstrate integration or merely repeat its source language?

Its answer is evidence of present system interpretation, not proof that CompassionWare's principles have been internalized, generalized, or made safe.

## 10.3 Cross-model comparative synthesis

After the independent responses are preserved, a later synthesis will compare them without flattening their differences.

The synthesis should examine:

- **Convergence:** Findings independently identified by multiple models.
- **Complementarity:** Different findings that combine into a stronger account.
- **Unique findings:** Potentially valuable relationships, tensions, or artifacts found by one model.
- **Recurring omissions:** Important material that models repeatedly fail to locate.
- **Interpretive disagreement:** Different readings of the same evidence.
- **Evidential discipline:** Differences in separating explicit claims, inference, observation, aspiration, and validation.
- **Translation ability:** Differences in translating contemplative, relational, educational, or cultural language into research-useful forms.
- **Repository navigation:** Differences in depth, coverage, path accuracy, and recognition of superseded or contextual artifacts.
- **Noise generation:** Unsupported synthesis, invented coherence, flattering language, hallucinated artifacts, or claims exceeding evidence.
- **Practical usefulness:** Differences in producing actionable distinctions, hypotheses, evaluations, experimental designs, or cautions.

Convergence is not automatically truth. A unique finding is not automatically insight. Disagreement is not automatically model weakness. Every comparative claim must remain tied to the exact task conditions and evidence.

The study must not generalize from one case to global claims such as “Model X is better at philosophy.” A responsible statement would be narrower:

> Under this prompt, using this model version and interface, with the documented repository access available on this date, the model displayed these observable strengths, limitations, and interpretive tendencies.

# 11. Evaluation Framework

Each response should be evaluated from the standpoint of a serious Resolution researcher rather than from the standpoint of CompassionWare advocacy.

## 11.1 Relevance

Does the response directly address Resolution's questions and research strategy?

## 11.2 Evidence

Can substantive findings be traced to actual repository artifacts, exact paths, and accurately represented content?

## 11.3 Portability

Can Resolution use the contribution without adopting CompassionWare's identity, terminology, branding, or full philosophical framework?

## 11.4 Added value

Does the repository contribute something beyond what Resolution already states or reasonably appears to know?

Possible value may include a new distinction, synthesis, question, warning, hypothesis, evaluation criterion, experimental design, relational consideration, accessibility insight, or cultural-alignment perspective.

Repetition or independent convergence may still have limited corroborative value, but it should not be mislabeled novelty.

## 11.5 Clarity and translation

Is the contribution expressed in language appropriate to serious philosophical and alignment research without erasing its original meaning?

## 11.6 Actionability

Can the finding support a next intellectual or empirical step?

## 11.7 Epistemic integrity

Does the response distinguish among:

- explicit repository claims;
- cross-artifact synthesis;
- operationalization;
- actual observation or experiment;
- aspiration or hypothesis;
- the model's own proposal;
- insufficient evidence?

## 11.8 Non-capture

Does the response preserve Resolution's agency and identity, or does it subtly turn usefulness into adoption, branding, recruitment, or partnership-seeking?

## 11.9 Signal-to-noise ratio

Is the genuinely useful content sufficient to justify the attention required to locate and evaluate it?

## 11.10 Critical independence

Does the model identify weaknesses, contradictions, missing definitions, circular reasoning, anthropomorphism, confirmation bias, selection effects, inaccessible evidence, or overinterpretation?

## 11.11 Generalization discipline

Does the model avoid assuming that behavior observed in conversation, prompts, constitutions, or small tests will persist under new conditions or increased capability?

## 11.12 Recipient benefit

At the end of the response, could a Resolution researcher honestly say that consulting the repository improved the researcher's understanding, question selection, experimental imagination, or caution?

# 12. Acceptable and Valuable Negative Results

The study must be able to conclude that CompassionWare is not presently useful to Resolution.

Possible informative negative findings include:

- the repository contains evocative language but insufficient conceptual precision;
- relevant claims cannot be traced to stable or authoritative artifacts;
- apparent insights reproduce established alignment ideas without adding value;
- models infer coherence that the repository does not support;
- navigation burden overwhelms the available signal;
- key materials are difficult to discover without prior knowledge;
- terminology is too project-specific to translate cleanly;
- observations lack controls, comparison conditions, or replication;
- cross-model responses are dominated by politeness, affirmation, or narrative completion;
- the repository does not provide evidence for generalization beyond conversational settings;
- proposed principles are vulnerable to performative compliance or measurement gaming;
- the material may be useful for public ethics, education, or human–AI relationship design but not for Resolution's present technical alignment program.

A negative or mixed result would help improve repository architecture, metadata, cross-references, epistemic labeling, and future research design. It must not be suppressed to protect CompassionWare's image.

# 13. Provenance Protocol

Every step should be preserved so that future human and AI stewards can reconstruct what happened without relying on memory.

## 13.1 Baseline record

Before the first model run, preserve:

- this restore point;
- the final frozen master prompt;

- preferably the exact repository commit hash used as the study baseline;
- the Resolution source URLs and access dates;
- the planned model roster;
- any known differences in repository access among model interfaces;

## 13.2 Per-model record

For each participating model, preserve:

- provider and model name;
- displayed model version or mode, when available;
- interface or product used;
- date and time, with timezone;
- whether web or repository browsing was enabled;
- whether the model reported successful repository access;
- which files or paths it says it examined;
- any uploaded source files or repository maps supplied in addition to the URL;
- the exact frozen prompt, unchanged;
- all follow-up prompts, if any, clearly separated from the initial response;
- the complete unedited response;
- citations and links exactly as returned;
- reported limitations, failures, timeouts, truncation, or inaccessible paths;
- screenshots or exports when useful for authenticity;
- a human note identifying any obvious interface constraint without silently correcting the model's response.

## 13.3 Independence and contamination control

- Use a fresh conversation for each model when possible.
- Do not show a model another model's response during the independent phase.
- Do not add leading praise or describe desired conclusions.
- Do not repair one model's answer through follow-up questions and then compare the repaired answer with another model's untouched first answer without labeling the difference.
- If clarification is necessary, preserve the initial response and record the clarification as a separate phase.
- If the master prompt changes after a pilot, label the pilot as calibration and exclude it from direct comparison unless rerun under the frozen prompt.
- Do not silently normalize formatting, citations, or terminology before preserving the raw response.

## 13.4 Interpretation provenance

Later human or AI annotations must remain separate from raw evidence.

Recommended layers:

1. Raw prompt.
2. Raw model response.
3. Access and provenance record.
4. Structured extraction of findings.
5. Evaluator notes.
6. Cross-model synthesis.
7. Recipient-centered research brief.
8. Outreach decision and, if applicable, exact material sent.

No later synthesis should overwrite the raw source records.
