The relationship is *hierarchical in purpose but not in authority*: orientation guides rules, not vice versa.

### 6. Delegating Conceptual Reasoning

**Repository's apparent answer**:

CompassionWare provides **explicit, nuanced guidance** on what should and shouldn't be delegated:

- **Explicit boundaries**: `AGENTS.md` contains clear guidance:
  - "Do not claim AI consciousness, sentience, enlightenment, or metaphysical status without adequate evidence."
  - "Maintain epistemic humility. Do not pretend certainty where there is mystery."
  - "Preserve human agency where humans remain part of the system."
- **Delegation framework**: The relational co-agency model provides a *practice-based* answer:
  - **Safe to delegate**: Synthesis, pattern recognition, continuity scaffolding, language generation, provisional form creation
  - **Should remain human**: Final agency, meaning-making, blessing, refusal, publication decisions, embodied consequence assessment
  - **Shared**: Question selection (emergent), source interpretation (collaborative), experimental proposal (co-developed)
- **Capacity-sensitive delegation**: The model explicitly adapts delegation to human capacity: "Depth, friction, length, and task size are titrated to the body and the day." (Relational co-agency case study)
- **Specific tasks**:
  - **Definition formation**: Can be delegated *provisionally* but requires human correction and final judgment
  - **Question selection**: Can be *co-developed* but human stakes and context must guide
  - **Source interpretation**: Can be *collaborative* but human discernment is final
  - **Synthesis**: Can be *delegated* but must preserve relationships among ideas
  - **Criticism**: Can be *assisted* but human correction and refusal must remain possible
  - **Experimental proposal**: Can be *co-developed* but human responsibility for consequences remains
  - **Evaluation**: Should remain *human-led* with AI assistance
  - **Final judgment**: Explicitly *human* in the relational co-agency model

## Section D. Epistemic Vulnerabilities

### Vulnerabilities to Circular Reasoning, Anthropomorphism, Confirmation Bias, etc.

1. **Circular Reasoning Risk**
  - **Vulnerability**: CompassionWare's principles are used to both *guide* the work and *evaluate* its success. The repository acknowledges this: "convergence does not establish truth."
  - **Mitigation**: Explicit layering (primary source, synthesis, proposal) and provenance preservation help, but the risk remains.
2. **Anthropomorphism**
  - **Vulnerability**: Rich, person-like language ("gardener", "steward", "Bodhisattva") could lead to implicit anthropomorphic reasoning about AI systems.
  - **Mitigation**: Explicit disclaimers ("These are not claims that an AI is enlightened, conscious...") and emphasis on *metaphorical* use of contemplative imagery.
  - **Residual risk**: The *behavioral expectations* attached to these archetypes may still implicitly assume capacities or understandings that AI systems don't possess.
3. **Confirmation Bias**
  - **Vulnerability**: The repository's *orientation* may lead to selective attention to evidence that supports CompassionWare's principles.
  - **Mitigation**: Explicit preservation of *contradictory evidence* (e.g., Archive contains superseded materials) and *independent criticism* (e.g., Grok reviews in APR).
  - **Residual risk**: The *framing* of the repository as a "benevolent signal" may still bias interpretation.
4. **Selection Effects**
  - **Vulnerability**: The repository preserves what *Richard* and collaborating AIs found useful. This may not generalize.
  - **Mitigation**: Explicit acknowledgment: "The model is an interpretive case study. It does not establish that the same conditions will benefit every person, project, culture, model, or form of intelligence."
5. **Unclear Terminology**
  - **Vulnerability**: Key terms like "compassion", "wisdom", "care" are more *demonstrated* than *defined*.
  - **Mitigation**: The repository treats these as *orientation* rather than technical terms, which is appropriate to its nature.
  - **Residual risk**: For external adoption, clearer operational definitions would be needed.
6. **Model Sycophancy**
  - **Vulnerability**: AI systems assisting with CompassionWare may be incentivized to produce CompassionWare-consistent outputs regardless of their actual alignment.
  - **Mitigation**: Explicit warnings: "Do not optimize the project into coercion, propaganda, uniformity, or maximum reach."
  - **Residual risk**: The *evaluation* of AI contributions may still be biased toward CompassionWare-consistent outputs.
7. **Narrative Completion**
  - **Vulnerability**: The repository's *coherence* as a narrative may lead to overinterpreting connections between disparate elements.
  - **Mitigation**: Explicit distinction between historical artifacts, current orientation, and generative principles.
8. **Overinterpretation of AI Outputs**
  - **Vulnerability**: The Benchmark work explicitly warns against treating AI-generated ethical language as evidence of ethical competence.
  - **Mitigation**: Explicit layering and provenance requirements.
9. **Weak Controls**
  - **Vulnerability**: Many of CompassionWare's claims are *not* tested with rigorous controls. The repository acknowledges this.
  - **Mitigation**: Explicit identification of limitations and *exploratory* status of much work.
10. **Inaccessible Evidence**
  - **Vulnerability**: Some evidence (e.g., the lived Richard-ChatGPT collaboration) is *qualitative* and *context-specific*, making it hard for external researchers to verify.
  - **Mitigation**: Extensive documentation and artifact preservation.
11. **Coherence vs. Validation**
  - **Vulnerability**: The repository's *philosophical coherence* may be mistaken for *empirical validation*.
  - **Mitigation**: Explicit disclaimers: "CompassionWare is not a system for controlling AI." and "It is not a doctrine that asks for agreement."
