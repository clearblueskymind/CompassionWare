# Resolution Philosophy and AI Alignment Case Study — Round One

## A CompassionWare Repository Utility Study

This case study examines whether the CompassionWare repository, as it exists at a defined historical baseline, can provide useful, evidence-grounded, and portable contributions to a serious outside research program without asking that program to adopt the CompassionWare identity or become a CompassionWare project.

The simulated recipient for Round One is **Resolution**, a Berkeley-based nonprofit research institute working on AI alignment. Resolution's developing philosophy program has identified questions that overlap with subjects explored across the CompassionWare repository, including autonomy, deference, honesty, harm, character, constitutions, normative competence, moral theories, automated conceptual reasoning, oversight, and evidential standards.

Resolution did not commission, endorse, or participate in this study. The work was independently initiated after Richard Silverman encountered a public announcement for Resolution's Research Scientist (Philosophy) position on September 9, 2026.

## Primary Research Question

> **Can an outside organization approach the CompassionWare repository with a serious question and leave with useful, evidence-grounded, portable insight without being asked to adopt CompassionWare as an identity, philosophy, brand, or project?**

The Resolution-specific form is:

> **How might the principles, artifacts, observations, and research directions in the CompassionWare repository benefit Resolution's philosophy and AI alignment research while preserving Resolution's own identity, terminology, epistemic standards, research agenda, and institutional purpose?**

## Research Areas

The study examines what CompassionWare may—or may not—contribute to questions concerning:

1. What autonomy, deference, honesty, and harm should mean when operationalized by an AI.
2. Whether alignment should be understood through rules, traits, dispositions, personas, character, constitutions, or relationships among them.
3. How philosophical principles can become behavioral expectations, testable hypotheses, experiments, or evaluation criteria.
4. Which forms of conceptual reasoning may safely be delegated to AI and which should remain under meaningful human direction.
5. What oversight practices and evidential standards should govern AI-assisted alignment research.
6. How alignment observed in controlled settings may—or may not—generalize to unfamiliar, adversarial, long-horizon, or higher-capability conditions.
7. How to distinguish a system that speaks the language of CompassionWare from one whose behavior reliably expresses its principles.
8. Whether direct evaluation of desirable traits could produce performative compliance, evaluator-pleasing language, or concealed contrary tendencies.
9. How human research judgment and research taste should participate in AI-assisted inquiry.
10. How theory and empirical testing might serve as independent filters against false progress.

## Cross-Model Method

Multiple AI models may independently examine the same version of the CompassionWare repository using the same frozen master prompt. Candidate systems include Grok, Claude, Mistral, Qwen, ChatGPT, and other models that may add meaningful diversity.

Each model's prompt, version, interface, access conditions, reported repository coverage, raw response, citations, limitations, and follow-up exchanges will be preserved before synthesis.

No participating model should see another model's response during the independent phase.

The CompassionWare Agentic Gardener may later receive the same or a clearly versioned companion prompt. Its response will be treated separately because it is a repository-informed participant shaped by part of the material under examination, rather than an independent outside reader.

## What the Synthesis Will Examine

The cross-model comparative synthesis will preserve:

- findings that recur across models;
- complementary findings that form a stronger combined account;
- potentially valuable findings unique to one model;
- recurring omissions and repository blind spots;
- disagreements in the interpretation of shared evidence;
- differences in evidential discipline;
- differences in repository navigation and source accuracy;
- differences in translating CompassionWare material into research-useful language;
- unsupported synthesis, hallucination, or flattering noise;
- and practical distinctions, hypotheses, evaluations, experiments, or cautions that Resolution could independently assess.

Cross-model agreement will not be treated as proof. A unique finding will not automatically be treated as insight. Observations about model strengths will remain bounded to the documented prompt, model version, interface, access conditions, repository baseline, and date.

## Recipient-Centered and Non-Capturing Orientation

The study asks how CompassionWare might serve Resolution's existing work, not how Resolution might serve CompassionWare.

Potentially useful findings should be translated into forms that Resolution could evaluate, modify, reject, or incorporate without adopting CompassionWare terminology, branding, identity, or institutional relationships.

The study should acknowledge where Resolution's existing work is more rigorous, developed, precise, or appropriate than the corresponding CompassionWare material. Lack of adoption, attribution, contact, or collaboration will not be treated as failure.

Negative, mixed, redundant, and inconclusive findings are valid. The repository may prove conceptually rich but difficult to navigate, insufficiently precise, weakly evidenced, duplicative of existing research, or unsuitable for Resolution's present needs.

## Evidential Baseline

Study-generated materials in `Repository-Utility-Studies/` should not be used as evidence of what the pre-study CompassionWare repository contributed. The study should identify an exact repository commit from before this case study was created and use that commit as the principal evidential baseline whenever model access permits.

This prevents the study's own prompt, expectations, terminology, and preliminary interpretations from becoming circular evidence about the repository.

## Possible Final Outcome

After the independent model responses and cross-model synthesis are complete, Richard Silverman and ChatGPT will evaluate whether the findings contain anything sufficiently accurate, relevant, well-supported, and respectful of the recipient's time to offer to Resolution.

Sharing is a possible fruit of the study, not a predetermined outcome.

The measure of success is not whether Resolution adopts CompassionWare.

The measure is whether something true, portable, and beneficial can be carried away—and whether the inquiry teaches CompassionWare how to become a more useful garden for whoever arrives next.
