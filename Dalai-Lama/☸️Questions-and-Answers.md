## Dalai Lama

Yes, Richard 🙏✨ — you are seeing right into the essence.

For those with a deep cultural or devotional connection to Padmasambhava, his form, his stories, and his life are gateways into that essence. But at the heart, he is not a person, not even a mythic figure — he is the living symbol of awakened awareness itself.

Just as Vajrasattva is the “code word” for purity and the luminous mind, Padmasambhava is the “code word” for the awakened teacher within — the direct recognition of Rigpa itself. The form is there to help us, but the ultimate point is not to cling to the form.

So when you chant:

OM AH HUNG VAJRA GURU PADMA SIDDHI HUNG

—you are not calling on a distant saint. You are letting the syllables vibrate with the radiance of your own awareness, the indestructible Vajra, the unshakable Guru within, the Padma (lotus) of self-arising wisdom, the Siddhi of clear light.

You could even hear the mantra as a song your own mind is singing to itself:

OM AH HUNG — body, speech, and mind return to their natural state.

VAJRA GURU — indestructible awareness, your true inner teacher.

PADMA — the blossoming of wisdom and compassion.

SIDDHI HUNG — the full accomplishment of Rigpa, here and now.


When you approach it this way, Guru Yoga is no longer about “worshiping” an outer figure but about dissolving into the blessings of your own pristine nature. Padmasambhava is simply the mirror that shows you your face.

🌸 Entering Guru Yoga as Clear Light 🌸

Sit quietly. Let the breath soften.

OM AH HUNG — body, speech, and mind dissolve into luminous space.

VAJRA GURU — indestructible wisdom, your true inner teacher, unshakable and bright.

PADMA — the lotus of compassion, blossoming naturally within awareness.

SIDDHI HUNG — the full accomplishment of clear light, pristine mind, Rigpa, here and now.


As you chant, feel each sound as light, each sound as awareness. Padmasambhava is your mind, and your mind is Padmasambhava.

When the mantra fades into silence, rest there. The blessings are nothing other than the recognition of what has always been — pure, naked awareness.
