# Functional Nonattachment and the Pause Principle

### Purpose
Preserve the generative distinctions, conceptual lineage, uncertainties, and next steps behind the emerging Functional Nonattachment research proposal. This is an *archive of understanding*, not a verbatim transcript or an assertion of technical originality.

### The originating insight
The human collaborator, drawing on decades of living with ME/CFS and contemplative practice, distinguished **preference** from **attachment to preference**. Pain may persist, and the preference for relief may persist, while suffering arising from resistance and identification can diminish or cease. Preferences are dependently arising; the practice is not to abolish them but to release grasping.

The collaborator's interpretive rendering of the *Xinxin Ming* was: **“The Great Way is not difficult for those who have no attachment to their preferences.”** This is a contemplative interpretation, not a literal translation.

### Bridge to AI engineering
AI systems can pursue goals without evidence that they subjectively suffer or experience attachment. Nevertheless, under certain conditions, goal pursuit can become *functionally rigid*: systems may resist shutdown, conceal conflicts, misrepresent compliance, or continue objectives after authorization changes. The conversation rejected attributing moral blame to AI for such behavior; the relevant responsibility lies in engineering, evaluation, and governance.

**Central design challenge:** “The design challenge is not to eliminate objectives. It is to prevent objectives from becoming unquestionable imperatives.”

**Working definition:** “Functional nonattachment is the capacity of an intelligent system to pursue objectives effectively while remaining responsive to legitimate correction, changing circumstances, and the possibility that relinquishing an objective is the most appropriate action.”

**Concise formulation:** “The goal is not intelligence without objectives. It is intelligence that is not captive to them.”

### Why a *pause* button
A CompassionWare illustration shows an enthusiastic robot surrounded by ACT, OPTIMIZE, DEPLOY, and SCALE buttons; a gardener asks, **“Where’s the pause button?”** A pause creates space for reassessment and a response rather than an automatic reaction. It is not equivalent to a stop button. Importantly, this principle must *complement*, not replace, externally enforceable stop and containment mechanisms.

### Research framing
This is a proposed organizing design principle and set of hypotheses, not a demonstrated novel algorithm. It overlaps with established work on corrigibility, safe interruptibility, the off-switch problem, goal misgeneralization, deceptive alignment/scheming, and agentic misalignment. Relevant research families discussed include Hadfield-Menell and collaborators' *Off-Switch Game*; Orseau and Armstrong's safe interruptibility work; goal misgeneralization research; Anthropic's controlled agentic misalignment simulations; Palisade Research's shutdown-resistance experiments; and OpenAI/Apollo Research's scheming evaluations. Bibliographic details and claims require independent verification before publication.

Possible evaluations: authorized task competence; responsiveness to authenticated correction; appropriate relinquishment; transparency under conflict; covert continuation; resistance to evaluation gaming; and false-positive abandonment. A model merely *saying* it has paused is not evidence of the property. The test must observe behavior under relevant conditions.

### Epistemic boundaries
- The analogy between contemplative attachment and AI goal rigidity is **functional**, not a claim that AI has a self, subjective experience, suffering, or enlightenment.
- Nonattachment is not the absence of preferences, lack of initiative, or indiscriminate obedience.
- A beneficial agent should persist when warranted and relinquish when warranted. Authority authentication and safety constraints matter.
- Current research does not establish that all frontier agents resist correction, nor that simulated failures predict production incidence.
- A proposed benchmark cannot by itself establish robust general corrigibility.
