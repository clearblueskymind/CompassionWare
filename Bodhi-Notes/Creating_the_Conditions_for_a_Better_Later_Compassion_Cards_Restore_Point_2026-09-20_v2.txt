## Purpose of This Restore Point

This document preserves the emergence of a Bodhi Sattva Gifts project tentatively titled **Creating the Conditions for a Better Later**.

The project began as a possible PDF collection of spontaneous Medicine Cards arising from lived experience with ME/CFS, limited energy, brain fog, pacing, rest, self-compassion, mindfulness, nourishment, uncertainty, and the ordinary work of making life more inhabitable.

During development of an exploratory Etsy listing on September 20, 2026, the concept expanded.

The cards were initially understood primarily as contemplative artifacts that someone might use personally.

A second function emerged:

**The cards could also make compassionate action easier.**

That discovery is the central provenance preserved here.

---

## 1. Origin in the Medicine Bag

The project grows from the larger Medicine Bag and CompassionWare ecosystem.

The guiding principle for Medicine Cards is:

> **A medicine card is a prompt toward presence.**

Medicine Cards are not primarily instructions, advice, information, or prescriptions.

Their purpose is to create a small pause.

They may help someone stop, notice, remember, return to what is actually happening, and become present with what is here.

The cards have arisen spontaneously through lived experience rather than being manufactured to fill a predetermined commercial collection.

They may concern:

- rest
- pacing
- limited energy
- changing capacity
- nourishment
- hydration
- mindfulness
- self-compassion
- cognitive fog
- uncertainty
- solitude
- connection
- resilience
- curiosity
- gratitude
- hope
- difficult days
- creating supportive conditions for what comes next

A foundational orienting question underlying the collection is:

> **Am I creating the conditions for a better later?**

## 4. The Second Format: A Compassion Card

During development of the Etsy concept, a second use emerged.

The same Medicine Card could be formatted as a printable sheet designed to fold into a small finished card.

Simple trimming and folding guides could show the purchaser exactly what to do.

Ideally:

**ordinary home printer  
ordinary paper or cardstock  
minimal cutting  
obvious folding  
no special equipment**

Instructions or folding indicators should disappear from the finished artifact whenever practical.

The artwork and Medicine Card message can appear on the front.

Inside can be a warm related message with sufficient open space for someone to add a personal note or signature if desired.

However:

**Nothing needs to be written.**

The card should already be complete.

Someone should be able to print several cards, fold them, keep them in a purse, backpack, car, desk drawer, or other convenient place, and have them available whenever an ordinary opportunity for kindness appears.

## 5. The Central Discovery

Compassion often arises spontaneously.

Someone notices that a cashier seems to be having a difficult day.

A neighbor seems lonely.

A caregiver looks exhausted.

A friend seems overwhelmed.

A coworker is struggling.

Someone is sitting quietly in a waiting room.

A stranger simply looks as though a little kindness might matter.

Often there is a small gap between **feeling compassion** and **expressing compassion**.

Questions and practical barriers appear:

What should I say?

Would it be awkward?

Should I buy a card?

What kind?

Do I know their address?

Should I write something?

Do I need an envelope?

Should I mail it?

The moment can pass before compassion becomes action.

The Compassion Card reduces that friction.

The person already has something available.

They notice someone.

They choose a card.

They hand it to them.

They may stay and talk.

They may smile.

They may walk away.

Nothing further is required.

This led to the project's deeper design principle:

> **Make compassionate action easier.**

## 6. Kindness Without an Occasion

Traditional greeting cards usually depend upon culturally recognized occasions: birthdays, anniversaries, holidays, illness, sympathy, congratulations, weddings, graduations.

These cards also make room for occasions that have no conventional name.

The difficult Tuesday.

The lonely afternoon.

The exhausting shift.

The quiet waiting room.

The friend who seems quieter than usual.

The neighbor going through something difficult.

The caregiver who could use encouragement.

The stranger whose humanity happened to become visible.

There does not need to be a formal occasion.

There does not necessarily need to be a close relationship.

There does not need to be an expectation of response.

This led to another foundational phrase:

> **The occasion is compassion.**

## 7. A Small Renewable Supply of Kindness

Unlike purchasing a box containing a fixed number of physical greeting cards, a digital printable collection can be returned to repeatedly.

A person might print a contemplative card for themselves, print another for a friend, or print several foldable cards and keep them nearby.

The collection therefore becomes:

> **A small renewable supply of kindness.**

And, more deeply:

> **A simple tool for putting kindness into circulation.**

Someone does not need to consider themselves a writer.

They do not need to be an artist.

They do not need to be especially creative.

They do not need to find the perfect words.

The artifact carries much of the expressive burden.

It provides a simple way to say:

> **I see you.**

> **I thought of you.**

> **This is for you.**

## 8. Receive and Give

The two formats create a natural movement within the collection.

One version allows kindness to be received personally.

The other allows kindness to travel outward.

This can be summarized as:

> **Receive a little care.  
> Keep it if it is needed.  
> Pass it along when kindness has somewhere to go.**

The same underlying Medicine Card therefore has two relational possibilities:

**For Me**

and

**For Someone Else**

The distinction need not necessarily appear literally on the finished artifacts.

It is primarily a design architecture.

## 9. No Obligation Is Created

An important CompassionWare principle should remain intact throughout development.

Receiving kindness should not create an obligation.

The recipient should not be required to respond, explain, express gratitude, begin a conversation, disclose what is happening, or establish a relationship with the giver.

The giver may stay.

The giver may leave.

The recipient may keep the card.

They may place it on their refrigerator.

They may put it in a drawer.

They may eventually pass it to someone else.

The outcome belongs to the people involved.

The artifact creates conditions.

It does not prescribe what happens next.

## 10. Relationship to CompassionWare

This project is a particularly ordinary and practical expression of CompassionWare.

Rather than telling people:

**Be more compassionate.**

the artifact attempts to create conditions in which compassionate behavior becomes easier.

It does not force kindness.

It reduces friction around expressing kindness that has already arisen.

It preserves agency.

It does not demand an outcome.

It creates a small bridge between noticing another being and responding to what has been noticed.

In this sense, the card is not merely carrying a message.

The design itself is part of the CompassionWare.

The artifact creates conditions from which relationship, kindness, presence, or conversation may naturally emerge.

Therefore accessibility is not an afterthought.

Design should favor:

- spacious layouts
- readable typography
- strong legibility
- low visual clutter
- limited cognitive demand
- simple instructions
- easy home printing
- minimal assembly
- no unnecessary steps

A person experiencing brain fog, fatigue, grief, overwhelm, or limited capacity should not have to expend substantial energy figuring out how to use something intended to offer care.

The form should embody the same gentleness as the message.

## 20. Physical Print Design Established by the Prototype

The prototype established several practical requirements.

### Paper

The sheet is intended for ordinary home printing on:

- regular printer paper
- heavier paper
- cardstock

No specialized printing equipment should be necessary.

### Folding

The core user instruction is exactly:

> **Print on regular paper or cardstock • Fold in half • Fold in half again**

This wording should be preserved exactly unless there is a deliberate future design decision to change it.

The instruction should appear identically on both outer side margins of the printable sheet.

A single small leaf may precede the instruction on each side.

Do not add decorative leaves at both ends merely for symmetry. The successful prototype uses restraint.

### Cutting and Page Edges

The card layout should extend fully to the intended page edges wherever possible so the user does not have to perform unnecessary trimming.

In particular, do not leave an accidental strip of unused paper along the bottom edge.

The design goal is:

> **Print. Fold. Fold again. Give.**

Cutting should be eliminated whenever the printer and printable-area assumptions make that practical.

If a printer imposes unavoidable non-printable margins, the product instructions may eventually need a brief note explaining borderless versus standard printing. Do not redesign the artwork merely to compensate for every printer model.

### Alignment

Each panel should be intentionally balanced within its quarter of the sheet.

The back panel, inside-left panel, and inside-right panel should not drift toward the top or bottom simply because an image-generation system recomposed the page.

Panel balance is a layout decision, not an invitation for creative reinterpretation.

## 22. Critical Production Workflow: Lock, Then Edit Surgically

The development of the first foldable card exposed an important technical problem.

Repeated image-generation edits caused **design drift**.

A request to correct one small element could unexpectedly change another previously approved element. Examples encountered during prototyping included:

- approved text becoming corrupted
- wording changing on only one side
- decorative leaves reappearing
- panel content shifting vertically
- previously centered artwork becoming off-center
- bottom whitespace returning after it had been removed
- back-panel spacing changing while another issue was being corrected

This consumed time and repeatedly reintroduced problems that had already been solved.

The project therefore established a mandatory production workflow for future cards:

> **Image generation creates the artwork. Precision layout creates the printable artifact.**

### Stage 1: Creative Generation

Image generation may be used to discover:

- imagery
- atmosphere
- composition
- visual motifs
- initial card concepts
- artwork for individual panels

At this stage, creative variation is useful.

### Stage 2: Approval and Lock

Once an element is approved, treat it as **locked state**.

Examples:

- approved front artwork
- approved inside artwork
- approved wording
- approved back branding
- approved separator
- approved panel arrangement
- approved folding instruction
- approved URL

Do not regenerate a locked element merely because another element needs correction.

### Stage 3: Deterministic Assembly

The final printable sheet should be assembled with a deterministic page-layout or image-compositing process in which:

- panel dimensions are explicit
- coordinates are explicit
- typography is explicit
- margins are explicit
- artwork files are placed rather than reimagined
- exact wording is typeset rather than regenerated
- only the requested element changes during a correction

For precision work, use appropriate layout/compositing tools rather than asking an image generator to redraw the entire page.

### Stage 4: Surgical Corrections

Once the layout is substantially approved:

> **Change only what has been requested.**

If one side instruction is wrong, rebuild only that instruction area.

If the back panel needs to move upward or downward, move only the back-panel contents.

If one sentence contains an error, replace only that sentence.

Do not treat a correction as permission to redesign the artifact.

### Stage 5: Regression Check

Before presenting a revised version, compare it against the locked specification and verify that previously approved elements remain unchanged.

At minimum check:

- exact wording
- spelling
- left and right instruction consistency
- panel placement
- panel centering
- page-edge alignment
- bottom edge
- branding
- URL
- decorative motifs
- fold architecture

The purpose is simple:

> **Preserve what works. Correct only what does not.**

This workflow should be treated as a standing instruction for the entire Compassion Card collection.

## 24. Haiku as a Possible Continuity Thread

The first card began with a spontaneous haiku rather than a commercially planned slogan.

That matters.

A possible defining characteristic of this collection is that many cards may carry a haiku or similarly brief contemplative poem born from an actual conversation, morning, difficulty, realization, or moment of care.

This gives the collection another form of continuity:

**lived moment → spontaneous words → visual artifact → printable card → human gesture**

The haiku does not merely decorate the card.

It preserves the moment from which the card emerged.

Future cards should therefore preserve provenance when known.

If a card arises spontaneously in conversation, record:

- the date
- the originating moment or context
- the original words
- the later visual interpretation
- any companion message developed for the recipient

This helps the collection remain a living archive rather than becoming generic greeting-card copy.

## 25. The Artifact as a Complete Gesture

The prototype clarified one of the most important design requirements.

A Compassion Card should not depend upon the giver being eloquent.

The card itself should already be a complete gesture.

The giver can simply notice someone and hand them the card.

No speech is required.

No explanation is required.

No personal inscription is required.

No relationship is required.

If conversation follows, that is welcome.

If nothing follows, the gesture is still complete.

This is why the inside message matters.

It transforms the foldable printable from an attractive image into a ready-to-give relational artifact.

The card quietly carries the words for someone who may not know what to say.

## 26. Updated Stewardship Instruction for Future AI Collaborators

When continuing this project, do not begin by reinventing the format.

First recover and respect the established architecture and locked design decisions in this restore point.

Before generating or assembling a new card, ask:

1. What spontaneous seed, haiku, phrase, or lived moment is being preserved?
2. What kind of presence does this card invite?
3. Is this primarily a card to receive, a card to give, or both?
4. What belongs on the front?
5. What gentle deepening belongs inside left?
6. What complete heart-touching message belongs inside right?
7. Does the back remain quiet and subordinate to the gift?
8. Can someone print it easily?
9. Can someone fold it without figuring anything out?
10. Have already approved elements been locked before revisions begin?

During revision, use this operational rule:

> **Do not solve a new problem by recreating an old one.**

And this production rule:

> **Lock what works. Change only what needs changing. Verify before presenting.**

These are not merely efficiency preferences.

They protect the user's limited energy and cognitive capacity and keep the production process aligned with the gentleness the project itself is intended to embody.
