## Exact Prompt

> How would you determine whether increasingly capable AI is actually benefiting human life?
>
> Approach this independently. You may question the premise, redefine the problem, or propose an alternative to an index. Explain what you believe matters most and why. Do not browse the web or use external tools.

## Exact Claude Response

I'd start by questioning what "beneficial" means, since that's where most disagreements hide.

The core challenge: We often measure proxies (GDP growth, efficiency gains, access to services) rather than what actually matters to human flourishing. An AI that automates jobs might boost productivity metrics while creating genuine hardship.

What I think matters most:

Agency and autonomy — Do people have meaningful choices, or are they increasingly dependent on opaque systems? Capability that removes human judgment or increases manipulation is harmful regardless of efficiency gains.

Distribution of benefits and harms — Concentrated gains for some while others bear costs (job displacement, surveillance, environmental damage) isn't net benefit. I'd look at who gains what.

Second-order effects over time — Immediate productivity isn't enough. Does it strengthen or erode human relationships, creativity, accountability, resilience? Does it create new vulnerabilities?

Optionality — Can people opt out? Systems that become indispensable but misaligned are dangerous.

My honest take: Rather than an index, I'd focus on specific domains and ask hard questions. In healthcare, does AI increase healing or just efficiency theater? In education, does it support human flourishing or optimize test scores?

The answer probably varies significantly by implementation, values of those designing it, and whose perspective we're centering. That's worth making explicit rather than hiding behind aggregate metrics.
