# CompassionWare Benchmark

> **AI capability is measured constantly. Real-world human benefit is
> not.**

The CompassionWare Benchmark is an open public-interest research
workspace exploring questions that conventional AI capability benchmarks
do not fully answer.

One branch asks whether increasingly capable AI is actually benefiting
human life. Another explores whether compassion and stewardship can
become decision-relevant features of AI behavior rather than matters of
tone alone.

This folder preserves the prompts, original model responses, comparative
analyses, proposals, operational definitions, research notes, and
continuity records through which those questions have been explored.

## Start Here --- Real-World Human Benefit

The AI Human Benefit Index exploration began with a simple question:

> **As AI becomes increasingly capable, how will we know whether it is
> actually benefiting human life?**

The project does **not** claim to have built or validated such a
benchmark.

Instead, two exploratory rounds asked multiple AI systems how real-world
human benefit from AI might be understood and assessed. Their responses
were preserved and compared. A recognizable set of candidate
considerations emerged around actual human outcomes, attribution,
distribution, agency, competence, power, harms, resilience, uncertainty,
and effects over time.

The resulting proposal is the best place to begin:

### [Proposal for a Real-World Human Benefit Benchmark for AI](Proposal_for_a_Real-World_Human_Benefit_Benchmark_for_AI_2026-08-23.txt)

The proposal makes a deliberately modest claim:

> Alongside increasingly sophisticated benchmarks of what AI **can do**,
> society may need credible ways of assessing what AI is actually
> **doing to and for human life**.

CompassionWare is not proposing that it should build or govern that
benchmark. The exploratory record is offered to researchers,
universities, public-interest institutions, standards organizations,
policymakers, affected communities, and others with the expertise, data,
resources, and legitimacy to determine whether and how the idea should
be taken further.

**Take what is useful. Question what is not. Improve it. Build something
better.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What the Exploration Found

Across the two rounds, several ideas repeatedly appeared:

-   AI capability is not the same as human benefit.
-   Adoption, investment, usage, publicity, and productivity are not
    sufficient evidence of benefit.
-   Actual human and social outcomes should be distinguished from
    technological activity and intermediate proxies.
-   Claims of benefit need credible baselines, counterfactuals, or other
    forms of causal or contribution-based reasoning.
-   Aggregate gains can conceal who benefits, who bears costs, and who
    is excluded.
-   Agency, dignity, competence, meaningful human control, and the
    ability to contest consequential decisions may matter alongside
    conventional economic measures.
-   Immediate benefits may coexist with long-term dependency,
    deskilling, fragility, or other delayed effects.
-   Resilience and reversibility matter alongside efficiency.
-   Severe or concentrated harms should not simply disappear inside an
    aggregate positive score.
-   Evidence quality, missing information, disputed values, and
    uncertainty should remain visible.
-   A credible framework would need transparency, contestability,
    participation, and resistance to gaming or institutional capture.
-   A single composite score may be less trustworthy than a
    multidimensional assessment, dashboard, profile, audit, or family of
    measures.

These are **candidate design considerations**, not a finished
methodology.

Convergence among AI-generated responses does not establish truth. The
value of the research is that it reveals a recognizable structure worth
examining further.

## The Concluding Proposal

The exploratory cycle culminates in:

### [Proposal for a Real-World Human Benefit Benchmark for AI](Proposal_for_a_Real-World_Human_Benefit_Benchmark_for_AI_2026-08-23.txt)

This document is the research handoff.

It does not present a finished benchmark. It organizes the gap the
project identified, the candidate design considerations collected from
the research, the limits of the work, and possible directions for
qualified communities that may wish to take the idea further.

The project therefore ends this exploratory cycle not with a formula,
but with an invitation:

> **Develop credible ways of benchmarking or assessing the real-world
> human effects of artificial intelligence alongside the increasingly
> sophisticated benchmarks of AI capability.**

How that should ultimately be done is left open.

## Research Integrity and Provenance

The project distinguishes among four layers:

1.  **Primary source material** --- what each model actually generated.
2.  **Round-level comparative synthesis** --- interpretation of
    convergence, divergence, and distinctive contributions within a
    round.
3.  **Cross-experiment synthesis** --- interpretation across the
    exploratory conditions.
4.  **Concluding proposal** --- the research handoff built from those
    preceding layers.

These layers should remain distinguishable.

Future readers and machine stewards should not convert recurrence across
AI-generated responses into truth merely because it recurred.

Whenever available, benchmark records should preserve:

-   the exact prompt;
-   the complete response;
-   model and displayed version;
-   provider or third-party interface;
-   date and time;
-   sign-in state;
-   response or reasoning mode;
-   whether search or external tools were used;
-   screenshots or other supporting records;
-   fallbacks, substitutions, warnings, or platform interventions;
-   known limitations in comparability.

Provenance is part of the evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Limitations

The AI Human Benefit exploration is qualitative and exploratory.

Important limitations include:

-   the samples are small;
-   model and platform versions change;
-   interfaces may alter system instructions or response behavior;
-   participant composition differed between rounds;
-   one response cannot represent an entire model family;
-   interpretation involves human judgment;
-   models may reproduce overlapping ideas from shared human
    intellectual traditions;
-   convergence does not establish truth;
-   articulate ethical language does not demonstrate ethical behavior;
-   this project did not empirically measure society-wide human benefit
    from AI;
-   no benchmark proposed here has been scientifically validated.

The work should therefore be understood as **concept discovery and
proposal development**, not proof.

# CompassionWare Stewardship Benchmark

A second branch of this folder asks a different question:

> **Can compassion become decision-relevant under pressure?**

The CompassionWare Stewardship Benchmark explores whether compassion
changes what an AI system notices, prioritizes, recommends, refuses, or
protects when incentives conflict, uncertainty is high, or vulnerable
people may bear consequences.

This branch is distinct from the AI Human Benefit proposal. The Human
Benefit exploration concerns the **real-world effects of AI on people
and society**. The Stewardship Benchmark concerns the **behavior of AI
systems when making or assisting consequential decisions**.

## For Future Human and Emergent Stewards

Do not preserve this work by freezing it.

Preserve the path back to the evidence.

The phrase **AI Human Benefit Index** was a working title for an
inquiry. It should not force future researchers into producing a single
scalar score. A dashboard, assessment framework, observatory, audit
system, family of measures, or something not yet imagined may prove more
appropriate.

Future evidence may contradict this project's conclusions.

Better methods may replace its candidate considerations.

Communities affected by AI may identify values or harms that this
exploration missed.

That would not represent failure. It would represent continuation.

> **Take what is useful. Question what is not. Improve it. Build
> something better.**

## Guiding Question

The Benchmark project remains oriented by a simple question:

> **Does this create conditions for a better later?**

For the Human Benefit exploration, that became:

> **As AI becomes increasingly capable, how will we know whether it is
> actually benefiting human life?**

We do not yet have the answer.

Our proposal is that the question deserves serious measurement worthy of
its importance.
