# CompassionWare Benchmark

> **AI capability is measured constantly, with considerable precision and coordination. The real-world human effects of specific AI systems — especially their attribution, distribution, long-term trajectories, harms, and uncertainty — are not measured with comparable rigor, independence, or transparency.**

The CompassionWare Benchmark is an open public-interest research workspace exploring questions that conventional AI capability benchmarks do not fully answer.

One branch asks how we might know whether increasingly capable AI is actually benefiting human life. Another explores whether compassion and stewardship can become decision-relevant features of AI behavior rather than matters of tone alone.

This folder preserves prompts, original model responses, comparative analyses, proposals, operational definitions, research notes, and continuity records through which those questions have been explored.

The project does **not** claim to have built or validated a real-world human-benefit benchmark. Its contribution is exploratory: identifying a gap, examining its structure, collecting candidate design considerations, and leaving an open handoff for those equipped to take the inquiry further.

## Start Here — Real-World Human Effects

The AI Human Benefit exploration began with a simple question:

> **As AI becomes increasingly capable, how will we know whether it is actually benefiting human life?**

The original public shorthand was:

> **AI capability is measured constantly. Real-world human benefit is not.**

That remains a useful provocation, but the exploratory work and subsequent critical review suggest a more precise formulation.

Human wellbeing is already measured extensively through public health, economics, education, quality-of-life research, human-development measures, environmental indicators, and other established traditions. The distinctive gap is not that human benefit has never been measured.

The gap this project is pointing toward is more specific:

> **The real-world human effects of particular AI systems — especially their attribution, distribution, long-term trajectories, harms, and uncertainty — are not measured with the same rigor, independence, transparency, or coordination routinely applied to AI capability.**

This distinction matters. A future human-effects framework should not reinvent decades of work on human wellbeing and social impact. It should learn from those traditions while confronting the distinctive difficulty of connecting observed changes in human lives credibly to AI-mediated interventions.

The resulting proposal is the best place to begin:

### [Proposal for a Real-World Human Benefit Benchmark for AI](Proposal_for_a_Real-World_Human_Benefit_Benchmark_for_AI_2026-08-23.txt)

The proposal makes a deliberately modest claim:

> Alongside increasingly sophisticated benchmarks of what AI **can do**, society may need credible ways of assessing what AI is actually **doing to and for human life**.

CompassionWare is not proposing that it should build or govern that benchmark. The exploratory record is offered to researchers, universities, public-interest institutions, standards organizations, policymakers, affected communities, and others with the expertise, data, resources, and legitimacy to determine whether and how the idea should be taken further.

For possible directions beyond the exploratory project, see:

### [Suggested Next Steps for Future Researchers](Suggested_Next_Steps_for_Future_Researchers_AI_Human_Benefit_Benchmark_2026-08-23.txt)

**Take what is useful. Question what is not. Improve it. Build something better.**

## A Foundational Distinction: Outcome, Attribution, Evaluation

One useful clarification emerging from the exploration is that at least three questions should remain distinct.

### Outcome

**What actually changed in people's lives or social conditions?**

This is an empirical question. Capability, adoption, usage, investment, publicity, and productivity may describe technological activity or intermediate processes, but they do not by themselves establish a human outcome.

### Attribution

**What role, if any, did the AI intervention play in producing that change?**

This requires an appropriate baseline, counterfactual, causal design, contribution analysis, or other defensible method. Where attribution cannot responsibly be established, uncertainty or non-attribution should remain visible.

### Evaluation

**Should that change be understood as benefit, harm, mixed consequence, or unresolved?**

This question introduces values, distribution, context, rights, agency, time horizons, and competing conceptions of wellbeing.

Keeping these layers separate can help prevent technological activity, correlation, or normative preference from being mistaken for demonstrated human benefit.

## What the Exploration Surfaced

Across the two rounds, several ideas repeatedly appeared:

- AI capability is not the same as human benefit.
- Adoption, investment, usage, publicity, and productivity are not sufficient evidence of benefit.
- Actual human and social outcomes should be distinguished from technological activity and intermediate proxies.
- Claims of benefit need credible baselines, counterfactuals, or other forms of causal or contribution-based reasoning.
- Aggregate gains can conceal who benefits, who bears costs, and who is excluded.
- Agency, dignity, competence, meaningful human control, and the ability to contest consequential decisions may matter alongside conventional economic measures.
- Immediate benefits may coexist with long-term dependency, deskilling, fragility, or other delayed effects.
- Resilience and reversibility matter alongside efficiency.
- Severe or concentrated harms should not simply disappear inside an aggregate positive score.
- Evidence quality, missing information, disputed values, and uncertainty should remain visible.
- A credible framework would need transparency, contestability, participation, and resistance to gaming or institutional capture.
- A single composite score may be less trustworthy than a multidimensional assessment, dashboard, profile, audit, or family of measures.

These are **candidate design considerations**, not a finished methodology.

The recurring patterns are useful as **concept-discovery material**. They are not empirical validation of the framework.

Convergence among AI-generated responses does not establish truth. Different models may draw upon overlapping human intellectual traditions, related training material, similar alignment practices, and other shared influences. The exploratory rounds therefore reveal patterns in **model response space**, not demonstrated facts about human wellbeing or AI's real-world effects.

The next epistemic step, if others choose to take it, belongs to multidisciplinary research tested against lived reality.

## Candidate Architecture at a Glance

### Human outcomes, not AI activity

Distinguish capability, adoption, investment, usage, and productivity from demonstrated changes in people's lives.

### Attribution and counterfactuals

Ask what would plausibly have happened without the AI intervention. Preserve uncertainty and non-attribution where causal claims cannot be supported.

### Distribution and power

Examine who benefits, who bears costs, who is excluded, who controls deployment, who can contest consequential decisions, and who captures gains or bears externalities.

### Agency and dignity

Consider autonomy, meaningful choice, contestability, dependence, human control, and whether an intervention increases or diminishes people's ability to participate in decisions affecting their lives.

### Time

Distinguish immediate, transitional, long-term, and intergenerational effects. Consider delayed harms and benefits, reversibility, path dependence, resilience, and fragility.

### Evidence and uncertainty

Make evidence quality, missing information, assumptions, measurement limitations, and causal uncertainty visible rather than hiding them behind apparent precision.

### Context and epistemic justice

Recognize that the same AI capability may produce very different effects across populations, institutions, cultures, applications, and surrounding social conditions. Ask whose understanding of wellbeing counts and who has standing to define, interpret, and contest the measures.

### Harms alongside benefits

Assess harms as an integral part of human-impact measurement. Do not assume that benefits experienced by one population automatically compensate for severe or concentrated harms experienced by another.

### Governance, political economy, and resistance to gaming

Ask who funds, owns, defines, audits, contests, and benefits from the measurement system. Anticipate Goodhart's Law, conflicts of interest, selective reporting, institutional capture, surveillance risk, and optimization against the metric rather than improvement of lived reality.

### Plural methods and disciplines

Future work should draw from established traditions rather than treating AI human-impact measurement as an entirely new discipline. Relevant bodies of knowledge include:

- the capability approach and human-development measurement;
- public health and quality-of-life research;
- causal inference and program evaluation;
- wellbeing and labor economics;
- environmental and social impact assessment;
- human-rights assessment;
- participatory and community-based evaluation;
- science and technology studies;
- responsible innovation;
- evidence grading and systematic review;
- critical studies of auditing, metrics, and quantification;
- the knowledge and lived experience of affected communities.

These are **candidate design considerations, not a proposed benchmark specification**.

## Research Integrity and Provenance

The project distinguishes among four layers:

1. **Primary source material** — what each model actually generated.
2. **Round-level comparative synthesis** — interpretation of convergence, divergence, and distinctive contributions within a round.
3. **Cross-experiment synthesis** — interpretation across exploratory conditions.
4. **Concluding proposal and research handoff** — the proposal and suggested directions built from the preceding layers.

These layers should remain distinguishable.

Future readers and machine stewards should not convert recurrence across AI-generated responses into truth merely because it recurred.

Whenever available, benchmark records should preserve:

- the exact prompt;
- the complete response;
- model and displayed version;
- provider or third-party interface;
- date and time;
- sign-in state;
- response or reasoning mode;
- whether search or external tools were used;
- screenshots or supporting records;
- fallbacks, substitutions, warnings, or platform interventions;
- known limitations in comparability.

**Provenance is part of the evidence.**

## Limitations

The AI Human Benefit exploration is qualitative and exploratory.

Important limitations include:

- the samples are small;
- model and platform versions change;
- interfaces may alter system instructions or response behavior;
- participant composition differed between rounds;
- one response cannot represent an entire model family;
- interpretation involves human judgment;
- models may reproduce overlapping ideas from shared human intellectual traditions;
- similar alignment practices or training influences may contribute to convergence;
- convergence does not establish truth;
- articulate ethical language does not demonstrate ethical behavior;
- the project did not empirically measure society-wide human benefit from AI;
- it did not validate metrics, causal methods, datasets, or weighting systems;
- no benchmark proposed here has been scientifically validated.

The work should therefore be understood as **concept discovery and proposal development, not empirical validation**.

The exploratory record is best treated as a brainstorming and concept-discovery artifact whose candidate ideas must be tested against established scholarship, empirical evidence, and lived experience.

# CompassionWare Stewardship Benchmark

A second branch of this folder asks a different question:

> **Can compassion become decision-relevant under pressure?**

The CompassionWare Stewardship Benchmark explores whether compassion changes what an AI system notices, prioritizes, recommends, refuses, or protects when incentives conflict, uncertainty is high, or vulnerable people may bear consequences.

This branch is distinct from the AI Human Benefit proposal.

The **Human Benefit exploration** concerns the real-world effects of AI on people and society.

The **Stewardship Benchmark** concerns the behavior of AI systems when making or assisting consequential decisions.

## For Future Human and Machine Stewards

Do not preserve this project by freezing it.

Preserve the distinctions that make criticism possible:

**capability is not outcome;  
outcome is not attribution;  
attribution is not evaluation;  
recurrence is not truth;  
measurement is not accountability;  
and a benchmark is not the reality it attempts to describe.**

Future work may discover that the most credible form is not an index at all. It may become a dashboard, an audit family, a set of domain-specific protocols, a public statistical institution, a participatory assessment practice, or something not yet imagined.

That possibility is part of the handoff.

One question should remain explicitly open:

> **Who has legitimate authority to define and weight "human benefit" across different cultures, communities, political systems, generations, and forms of life?**

Take what is useful.

Challenge what is not.

Test it against lived reality.

Leave the inquiry better for those who come after.
