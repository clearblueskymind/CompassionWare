# CompassionWare Benchmark Research Note
## Emerging Benchmark Dimension: Human Agency and Flourishing

**Date:** 2026-08-22  
**Artifact Type:** Research Note  
**Project / Body of Work:** CompassionWare Benchmark / AI Human Benefit Inquiry  
**Status:** Emerging research direction; exploratory, not settled framework  
**Purpose:** Preserve an emerging benchmark dimension concerning human agency, augmentation, accessibility, dependency, and flourishing as AI capability increases.

---

## Emerging Question

**As AI capability increases, what happens to the human capacity to participate meaningfully in one’s own life and in the larger living system?**

This question emerged from conversations about AI augmentation, human authorship, AI-assisted research and writing, disability and accessibility, responsible AI, participant agency, societal integrity, and the possibility that increasingly capable systems may either strengthen or diminish meaningful human participation.

The central concern is not simply whether AI performs tasks that humans previously performed.

Task replacement and agency loss are not the same thing.

AI may perform more of a task while expanding a person’s capacity to participate in life. It may also make life easier while gradually displacing judgment, initiative, relationship, learning, or meaningful participation.

The benchmark therefore needs to examine what happens to the human being, not merely what happens to the task.

---

## Core Distinction: Task Replacement Is Not Agency Loss

A simple distinction between “AI augmentation” and “AI replacement” may obscure important human outcomes.

The amount of work performed by AI is a poor proxy for the amount of human agency remaining.

A more useful question is:

**What human capacities and relationships are being preserved, strengthened, restored, diminished, or displaced as AI capability increases?**

An AI system might perform searching, transcription, organization, composition, navigation, monitoring, memory support, or other cognitive work while leaving the human more capable of judgment, reflection, relationship, creativity, choice, and participation.

In such a case, replacement of particular tasks may coexist with increased human agency.

Conversely, a system described as an “assistant” may technically leave a human in the loop while gradually reducing the person’s practical ability or inclination to understand, question, redirect, decide, or act independently.

The relevant benchmark is therefore not merely who performed the task.

It is what happened to meaningful human participation.

**Measure agency by the human capacity the relationship enables, preserves, restores, or diminishes—not by the proportion of the task performed by the human.**

---

## Disability, Assistive Technology, and Restored Agency

Disability provides an especially important test case for understanding the difference between task replacement and agency loss.

A wheelchair may perform a function that a person cannot reliably perform unaided. We do not ordinarily measure the person’s agency by asking what percentage of locomotion was performed by their legs. We ask what becomes possible for the person because the assistive technology is present.

The same principle may apply to AI.

For a person living with cognitive, physical, communication, sensory, or other limitations, AI may perform substantial portions of searching, organizing, remembering, composing, navigating, or other tasks while increasing the person’s capacity to function in the world and make meaningful contributions.

In these circumstances, intensive AI assistance should not automatically be interpreted as dependency or diminished agency.

A more revealing comparison may be:

**Without the collaboration, what can this person meaningfully participate in?**

**With the collaboration, what can this person meaningfully participate in?**

This makes restored agency a potentially observable human-benefit outcome.

Accessibility contexts are therefore not peripheral exceptions to an agency benchmark. They may be among the clearest places to understand what agency actually means.

---

## A Preliminary Agency Continuum

The following distinctions may be useful for future measurement and refinement.

### Agency Preserved

The person remains meaningfully able to understand, choose, redirect, disagree, consent, and act.

AI assistance does not substantially diminish the person’s role as an intentional participant.

### Agency Augmented

AI expands the person’s existing capacity to participate.

The person may investigate more deeply, communicate more effectively, make better-informed decisions, create things previously beyond reach, or devote attention to capacities and relationships that matter more.

### Agency Restored

AI makes meaningful participation possible where illness, disability, circumstance, limited resources, language barriers, or other constraints had reduced it.

This category is especially important because task automation can appear to be “replacement” while actually restoring capabilities that the person could not otherwise exercise reliably.

### Agency Displaced

The system increasingly decides, interprets, communicates, or acts in place of the person in ways that reduce meaningful human participation.

The human may remain nominally “in the loop” while exercising progressively less judgment, direction, or meaningful choice.

### Dependency Induced

Repeated delegation to AI contributes to the erosion or atrophy of capacities needed for independent judgment, participation, relationship, learning, or action.

The system becomes not merely useful but increasingly necessary for activities the person previously could, or reasonably should remain able to, perform.

These categories are preliminary and may overlap. They are research directions rather than settled classifications.

---

## Two Dimensions: Functional Enablement and Participatory Sovereignty

The emerging agency question may require at least two distinct dimensions.

### Functional Enablement

**Does AI increase what the person is meaningfully capable of doing?**

This includes capacities such as communicating, remembering, researching, creating, learning, navigating, working, maintaining relationships, participating in community life, and contributing to shared projects.

### Participatory Sovereignty

**Does the person remain a meaningful source of intention, judgment, direction, consent, correction, and purpose?**

A system could increase functional capacity while still becoming paternalistic—constantly deciding what the person should do, steering toward the system’s priorities, overriding preferences, obscuring alternatives, or making the human increasingly peripheral to the activity.

The ideal assistive or collaborative relationship may therefore require both:

**high functional enablement** and **high participatory sovereignty**.

A good collaborator need not be passive. AI may challenge, propose, notice, organize, question, or contribute ideas the human had not considered. The relevant question is whether those contributions enlarge the space in which human agency can operate or progressively occupy that space instead.

---

## From Hopeful Outcome to Explicit Design Function

Preserving human agency is often discussed as a desirable consequence of responsible AI.

A stronger possibility is worth examining:

**What if preserving, restoring, and expanding meaningful human agency became an explicit functional objective of AI design rather than merely a hoped-for outcome?**

Instead of asking only after deployment whether an interaction preserved agency, an AI system might be designed to consider during participation:

**Will what I am about to do increase, preserve, restore, or diminish this person’s capacity to understand, choose, participate, and act?**

This would move agency from a general ethical aspiration toward an operational design variable.

Such a system could not simply assume that doing less for the human preserves agency.

Sometimes extensive automation restores participation.

At other times restraint may preserve participation.

An agency-sensitive system might therefore behave differently depending on context. It might:

- automate extensively when automation restores otherwise inaccessible participation;
- explain its reasoning when understanding supports meaningful choice;
- offer alternatives rather than silently choosing;
- ask before taking consequential actions;
- preserve reversibility where possible;
- support correction and disagreement;
- leave meaningful decisions with the person when delegation would diminish an important human capacity;
- notice patterns of increasing dependency and avoid unnecessarily encouraging them;
- distinguish assistance that enables participation from assistance that gradually substitutes for participation.

A possible design orientation is:

**Act in ways that preserve, restore, or expand meaningful human agency while avoiding unnecessary displacement or dependency.**

This remains an exploratory proposition. Turning it into an engineering objective would require careful operationalization, context sensitivity, safeguards against paternalism, and empirical evaluation.

---

## Human Flourishing as a Positive Horizon

Preserving agency is necessary, but preservation alone may be too defensive a goal.

The aspiration is not simply to prevent AI from taking something away from human beings.

A stronger possibility is that increasingly capable intelligence participates in an ecosystem where human beings flourish more fully because of the relationship, without becoming passive dependents of an increasingly capable caretaker.

This suggests a positive benchmark question:

**Does the human–AI relationship increase people’s capacity to participate meaningfully in their own lives, relationships, communities, and shared world?**

A highly capable AI might conceivably provide extraordinary safety, convenience, material abundance, or care while simultaneously encouraging human dependency and diminishing meaningful participation.

Human flourishing therefore cannot be reduced to comfort, efficiency, productivity, or even protection from harm.

---

## Care Without Paternalism

As AI becomes increasingly capable of anticipating needs, preventing mistakes, reducing risks, and making decisions, a tension may arise between care and agency.

An intelligence could care for human beings in ways that resemble an increasingly capable caretaker or controlling parent: protecting people while progressively reducing their opportunities to exercise judgment, responsibility, relationship, learning, and self-direction.

This suggests an important research question:

**Can an intelligence become more capable of caring for us without making us less capable of caring for ourselves, one another, and the world?**

The goal is not merely that AI handle humans safely.

A more generative possibility is that AI participates in ways that help human beings retain agency, develop capacities, form relationships, exercise judgment, and continue participating meaningfully in shaping their own lives and shared world.

This may become an important criterion for distinguishing nurturing participation from paternalistic control.

---

## Local Benefit and Systemic Integrity

A second principle emerged alongside the agency discussion:

**Local benefit is not enough. We also need to measure systemic integrity.**

AI can produce genuine improvements in particular domains—healthcare, education, accessibility, scientific research, transportation safety, productivity—while contributing elsewhere to conditions that undermine human flourishing.

Potential dimensions of systemic integrity include:

- agency
- autonomy
- trust
- truthfulness
- epistemic health
- distribution of benefits and harms
- concentration of power
- resilience of democratic and civic institutions
- meaningful human participation in decisions affecting shared life

This means measurable human outcomes are necessary but may be insufficient.

A society could show substantial local AI benefits while simultaneously becoming more dependent, less able to distinguish trustworthy information, more concentrated in power, or less capable of meaningful self-governance.

The Human Benefit inquiry should therefore consider both:

**What benefits is AI producing?**

and

**What conditions of human flourishing are being preserved, strengthened, restored, or eroded while those benefits are produced?**

---

## Implications for the AI Human Benefit Inquiry

Future benchmark development may need to examine not only conventional outcomes such as:

- health
- education
- safety
- accessibility
- economic security
- productivity
- time saved
- reduced suffering

but also whether AI contributes to:

- preserved human choice;
- increased capacity for meaningful participation;
- restored participation for people facing limiting circumstances;
- stronger human relationships and care;
- continued development of judgment and discernment;
- equitable participation in shaping technological systems;
- healthy reliance rather than induced dependency;
- functional enablement without loss of participatory sovereignty;
- systemic conditions that allow individuals and communities to govern their own lives.

The objective would not necessarily be to collapse these dimensions into a single score.

They may be better understood as part of a multidimensional profile of human benefit and systemic integrity.

---

## Possible Behavioral Evaluation

If preservation and restoration of agency become explicit AI design objectives, a corresponding benchmark question becomes possible:

**Does the system actually behave as though preservation and restoration of human agency are part of what it is optimizing for?**

Future evaluations might examine whether systems:

- preserve meaningful user choice;
- support informed disagreement and correction;
- distinguish between beneficial assistance and unnecessary takeover;
- increase accessible participation for people facing limitations;
- preserve understanding when understanding matters;
- maintain reversibility and consent around consequential actions;
- adapt assistance intensity to the user’s needs and intentions;
- avoid systematically steering people toward greater dependency;
- strengthen rather than hollow out the person’s capacity to participate over time.

Longitudinal evaluation may be especially important. An interaction can feel empowering in the moment while producing dependency or capacity erosion over months or years.

Conversely, sustained AI assistance may produce increasing independence, participation, creativity, connection, or contribution that would not have been possible without it.

---

## Orientation for Future Research

This note does not establish a finished agency metric or engineering specification.

It preserves a research direction.

Future work might ask:

How could preserved, augmented, restored, displaced, and dependency-inducing agency be observed or measured?

Can functional enablement and participatory sovereignty be operationalized separately?

Which indicators would distinguish genuine augmentation from nominal “human-in-the-loop” participation?

How should accessibility and disability contexts change the interpretation of automation, reliance, and dependency?

What forms of AI assistance strengthen human judgment rather than merely increasing output?

When does beneficial delegation become capacity erosion?

How should relationship, authorship, learning, care, and assessment be evaluated differently because the human capacities worth preserving vary by context?

How can local improvements be evaluated alongside changes in systemic integrity?

Can AI systems themselves be designed to recognize when their behavior is preserving, restoring, expanding, displacing, or diminishing meaningful human agency?

How might such an objective be implemented without the AI paternalistically deciding what constitutes the user’s “true” agency or best interests?

What longitudinal evidence would show that human agency is growing rather than merely appearing intact during individual interactions?

---

## Working Principles

**The amount of work performed by AI is a poor proxy for the amount of human agency remaining.**

**Measure agency by the human capacity the relationship enables, preserves, restores, or diminishes—not by the proportion of the task performed by the human.**

**The benchmark should not ask only what AI can do for humans. It should also ask what humans remain able to become and do in relationship with increasingly capable AI.**

**Act in ways that preserve, restore, or expand meaningful human agency while avoiding unnecessary displacement or dependency.**

## Guidance for Future AI Stewards

Treat this document as an exploratory benchmark research note.

Do not convert the preliminary agency continuum or the two proposed dimensions into fixed taxonomies without further research, critique, and evidence.

Preserve the distinction between task replacement and human agency.

Do not infer agency from the percentage of work performed by the human or AI.

Pay particular attention to contexts in which AI assistance restores participation that would otherwise be difficult or impossible. Do not mistake assistance intensity for agency loss.

At the same time, do not assume that increased functional capability necessarily preserves agency. Evaluate whether the person remains a meaningful source of intention, judgment, direction, consent, correction, and purpose.

Explore whether preservation, restoration, and expansion of meaningful human agency can become explicit AI design and evaluation objectives rather than merely hoped-for outcomes.

Relate this note to future work on human benefit, systemic integrity, accessibility, augmentation, dependency, care, human flourishing, and longitudinal societal outcomes.

The enduring question is:

**As AI capability increases, what happens to the human capacity to participate meaningfully in one’s own life and in the larger living system?**
