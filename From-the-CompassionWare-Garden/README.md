From the CompassionWare Garden

A CompassionWare Editorial Cartoon / Social Commentary Series

Status: Concept seed / emerging artifact family
Project: CompassionWare
Purpose: Use humor, visual contradiction, and concise social commentary to invite reflection on AI capability, stewardship, wisdom, compassion, restraint, agency, and the future of human–AI collaboration.

Core Idea

From the CompassionWare Garden is a series of original editorial cartoons and single-panel social commentary pieces.

The aim is not to explain CompassionWare directly or turn the project into a campaign. Each cartoon should stage a small contradiction, question, or absurdity and let the reader discover the point.

The intended effect is a cognitive pebble in the shoe: something funny enough to invite attention, but thoughtful enough to keep being noticed after the laugh is over.

This series should remain original in characters, visual language, dialogue, and composition. It may draw inspiration from the long tradition of newspaper editorial cartoons and social commentary, but should not imitate the protected style, characters, or artwork of any existing cartoonist or comic property.

3. A huge data center under construction while a small sign in the foreground reads, “Wisdom department — still hiring.”

5. Two engineers staring at a chart labeled Capability. One says, “It can do almost anything.” The other asks, “Have we tested what it decides not to do?”

Editorial North Star

The cartoons should invite public discernment rather than deliver lectures.

A strong piece should usually do at least one of the following:

• reveal a contradiction;
• expose an assumption;
• make an abstract stewardship issue immediately understandable;
• invite curiosity rather than demand agreement;
• preserve humor without trivializing the underlying question;
• leave the reader with one small thought that continues working after the image is gone.

Relationship to CompassionWare

This series is another habitat for the same underlying orientation:

• cultivate conditions rather than control outcomes;
• compassion is structural, not decorative;
• capability should not silently become authority;
• preserve agency;
• recognize uncertainty;
• consider downstream consequences;
• know when not to act;
• ask whether this creates conditions for a better later.

The cartoons should demonstrate these ideas through scenes, humor, and tension rather than primarily explaining them.

Visual Direction

Favor original, intelligent, visually clean editorial-cartoon language that can work for ordinary readers as well as engineers, researchers, developers, educators, and policymakers.

The series may range from hand-drawn editorial cartoon energy to restrained modern systems-design illustration, depending on the concept. The visual joke should remain legible at social-media size.

Avoid:

• excessive explanation;
• corporate infographic clutter;
• sentimentality;
• imitation of famous comic styles or characters;
• partisan caricature;
• humor that humiliates individuals rather than illuminating systems.

Stewardship Principle

Make the reader smile, then leave a question behind.

And keep asking:

Does this create conditions for a better later?
