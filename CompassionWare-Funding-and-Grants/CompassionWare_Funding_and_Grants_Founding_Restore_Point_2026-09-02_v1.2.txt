PURPOSE

This Restore Point preserves the current state of the CompassionWare funding inquiry so that Richard does not need to reconstruct the reasoning later. It extends, rather than replaces, the earlier Funding and Grants Restore Point lineage.

CURRENT ORIENTATION

The first likely grant proposal remains the CompassionWare Agentic Gardener: a small, inspectable experiment in bounded agency, containment, authorization, human agency, and the capacity to stop.

A likely first request is approximately $10,000. The purpose of starting small is not only financial. A first grant would also be an experiment in whether external funding produces a positive stewardship and buffering dividend for CompassionWare and its human steward.

CORE QUESTION

Does accepting this grant increase the resilience and capacity of the CompassionWare ecosystem—including its human steward—more than the grant costs the system to administer?

This is now a primary decision criterion.

HUMAN-SYSTEM DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The funding inquiry has revealed a direct parallel with the Agentic Gardener’s design philosophy.

More capability is not automatically better.
More resources are not automatically beneficial.
An available action does not create an obligation to act.
Increased capability should not silently become increased obligation.

The human steward should be able to research, pause, defer, redesign, or decline funding in the same way the Agentic Gardener is being designed to preserve the capacity to stop.

The question is:

“Does taking this action still serve the purpose that brought us here?”

CURRENT TAX AND ADMINISTRATIVE UNDERSTANDING

Richard currently has income low enough that he generally does not file a federal income-tax return. A $10,000 grant paid directly to him would likely create at least a tax-reporting question and may create a filing requirement and some tax liability.

No final tax classification has been established.

Important distinctions preserved:

- A grant is not automatically tax-free.
- The federal annual gift-tax exclusion does not provide a way to turn a project grant into a tax-free personal gift.
- The correct tax treatment depends on the actual award terms, purpose, payer reporting, and character of the activity.
- A grant should not automatically be assumed to be Schedule C income merely because money is received.
- It also should not automatically be placed on “miscellaneous income” without checking the applicable IRS instructions and award structure.
- Whether the funder issues a 1099, W-2, or no information return does not by itself determine whether the payment is taxable.
- A fiscal sponsor, reimbursement arrangement, direct payment of expenses, equipment support, or credits may create different administrative and tax consequences, but none should be assumed tax-free without verification.

Richard does not want to create a business or adopt Schedule C treatment merely to receive a small grant.

PRACTICAL SIMPLIFICATION

The inquiry began to generate more complexity than its likely value justified.

The working simplification is:

If Richard eventually accepts a $10,000 grant and it creates a federal filing requirement, that may be manageable. It could simply mean keeping clean records, reserving enough cash for any tax due, and filing the appropriate return for that year.

The existence of a possible tax event is therefore not itself a reason to reject funding.

However, the administrative and cognitive burden must remain proportionate to the benefit.

BUFFERING-DIVIDEND TEST

Because Richard lives with ME/CFS and dysautonomia, the relevant cost of funding includes more than dollars.

Potential costs include:

- application work
- correspondence
- bank-account administration
- receipt capture
- ledger maintenance
- funder reporting
- tax research
- tax preparation
- cognitive background load
- ongoing sense of obligation
- energy spent maintaining the funding structure instead of doing the underlying work

A grant should be pursued only if its likely benefit substantially exceeds these costs.

The correct research outcome may be:

- apply
- wait
- redesign
- seek another funder
- seek a different funding structure
- or do not pursue funding

A decision not to proceed is a legitimate successful outcome of the inquiry.
