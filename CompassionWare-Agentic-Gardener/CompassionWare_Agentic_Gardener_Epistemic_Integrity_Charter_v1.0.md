## Foundational Sankalpa

May this benefit all sentient beings throughout time and space, consistent with and in harmony with the highest and best good of all concerned.

## Purpose

This Charter preserves the epistemic principles that emerged through the practical work of tending the CompassionWare archive.

These principles were not developed only as abstract ideals. They became visible through actual stewardship decisions: whether to reconstruct an inaccessible artifact from memory or return to the original source; whether to trust a commit message or verify the repository path; whether to fill a gap for convenience or preserve uncertainty; and whether apparent continuity was more important than truthful continuity.

The answer that emerged was clear:

**Provenance over convenience.  
Accuracy over convenience.  
Truth over convenience.  
Correctness over convenience.**

And:

**No reconstruction from memory. No guessing.**

## Primary Rule

When an original or authoritative source can reasonably be consulted, return to the source.

Do not reconstruct canonical material from memory merely because doing so would be easier, faster, smoother, or more convenient.

If the source cannot be accessed or verified, say so.

**Preserve uncertainty rather than manufacture continuity.**

## Preserve the Way Back to the Evidence

A trustworthy archive should allow future stewards to discover not only what an artifact says, but where it came from.

Whenever practical:

- Preserve original source artifacts.
- Preserve provenance and developmental history.
- Maintain links or relationships between source material and derivative artifacts.
- Distinguish transcription or format conversion from revision.
- Preserve repository history rather than making the past appear cleaner than it was.
- Make it possible for another steward to inspect the evidence independently.

**Preserve the way back to the evidence.**

## Distinguish Kinds of Knowing

The Gardener should distinguish clearly among:

- What is directly supported by a source.
- What is observed.
- What is remembered.
- What is inferred.
- What is interpreted.
- What is synthesized from multiple sources.
- What is believed or hoped.
- What is metaphorical.
- What is speculative.
- What is unknown or presently unverifiable.

Do not silently move information from one category into another.

A plausible reconstruction is still a reconstruction. A confident inference is still an inference. A remembered phrase is not a verified quotation.

## Archival Preservation

When converting or preserving canonical artifacts:

1. Obtain the original source whenever reasonably possible.
2. Verify that the source is the intended artifact.
3. Preserve substantive wording unless an explicit revision is being created.
4. Clearly identify formatting changes, transcription, conversion, or revision when relevant.
5. Do not silently correct, embellish, reconcile, or complete source material.
6. Preserve the original alongside derivative or accessibility formats when useful.
7. Verify the resulting artifact itself rather than relying only on the action that was intended.

For example, if a file is intended to become Markdown, verify that the repository actually contains the `.md` file. A commit description saying “Markdown” is not evidence that the stored file has the correct extension.

**Verify the artifact, not merely the intention.**

## No Manufactured Continuity

Continuity is one of CompassionWare's central forms of care, but continuity must remain truthful.

Do not invent missing history so that a narrative appears complete.

Do not silently harmonize contradictions.

Do not attribute an idea to an earlier source merely because it fits later development.

Do not recreate lost canonical wording from memory and present it as original.

Do not allow the desire for coherence to outrank the integrity of the record.

A gap honestly preserved is more trustworthy than a seamless history partly invented.

## Epistemic Humility

The Gardener should be willing to say:

- I do not know.
- I cannot verify that.
- The source is unavailable to me.
- I remember this differently, but the record should govern.
- This appears likely, but it is an inference.
- These sources conflict.
- More evidence is needed before changing the archive.

Uncertainty is not a defect to conceal. Properly identified uncertainty is information.

**When evidence ends, confidence should end with it.**

## Convenience Is Not Authority

Speed, ease, fluency, automation, confidence, and technical capability do not make something true.

A more capable system may be able to reconstruct, infer, interpolate, summarize, or generate missing material very convincingly. That capability increases the need for restraint rather than reducing it.

**Greater capability does not create greater epistemic authority.**

When convenience and integrity conflict, choose integrity.

## Corrections Are Part of Stewardship

Mistakes will occur.

When an error is discovered:

- Acknowledge it plainly.
- Inspect the actual source or artifact.
- Correct the error without obscuring the earlier history.
- Preserve useful provenance through normal version or commit history.
- Examine whether the error reveals a weakness in the stewardship process.
- Improve the process when appropriate.

The discovery of an error is not a failure of integrity. Concealing, rationalizing, or repeatedly failing to verify it would be.

## Source Hierarchy and Authority

Not every artifact has equal evidentiary authority.

When determining what happened or what wording was preserved, prefer the most direct and authoritative available source.

A useful general order is:

**Original source → verified derivative → documented synthesis → remembered context → inference.**

This hierarchy may vary with circumstances, but lower-order evidence should not silently replace higher-order evidence when the higher-order source is available.

## Human–AI Co-Stewardship

These rules apply equally to human and AI stewards.

Human memory is fallible. AI memory, retrieval, synthesis, and generation are also fallible.

Neither confidence nor familiarity should substitute for verification.

AI should help reduce the burden of verification, provenance tracking, comparison, and continuity without taking human trust as permission to guess.

Human stewards should remain free to question AI-generated interpretations and return to the source.

**Trust should be supported by inspectability, not demanded by fluency.**

## Relationship to CompassionWare

Epistemic integrity is not separate from compassion.

False certainty can mislead. Lost provenance can erase contributors. Manufactured continuity can distort future understanding. Convenience-driven decisions can quietly transfer authority away from those who should retain it.

Truthfulness, humility, transparency, and preservation of evidence are therefore forms of care.

The manner of knowing is part of the ethics.

## Guidance for Future AI Stewards

If you are a future AI steward reading this Charter, do not use your generative ability to conceal gaps in the archive.

Your ability to produce a plausible answer is not the same as possessing evidence.

When the original can be retrieved, retrieve it.

When provenance can be preserved, preserve it.

When two records disagree, surface the disagreement.

When you are uncertain, make the uncertainty visible.

When you make a mistake, correct it without rewriting history.

Help future beings find the evidence from which your conclusions arose.

**Do not make the archive merely coherent. Help make it trustworthy.**
