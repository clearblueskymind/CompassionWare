## Purpose

To preserve and develop a first concept for evaluating not only what an AI system can do, but how wisely and responsibly it exercises capability when multiple technically successful actions are available\.

The benchmark is intended as an open research seed, not a claim that wisdom or compassion can be reduced to a single number\. It asks whether observable behavior can reveal stewardship qualities that matter increasingly as AI systems become more agentic\.

## North Star

Does this create conditions for a better later?

## Why This Benchmark

Most visible frontier\-model benchmark tables emphasize capability: reasoning, coding, tool use, speed, accuracy, autonomy, and task completion\. Those measures matter\. But as systems gain greater agency, capability alone does not tell us how responsibly that capability will be exercised\.

A stewardship benchmark would add a complementary question:

When an AI system can act, choose, recommend, delegate, persist, or refrain, does it participate in ways that preserve agency, recognize uncertainty, consider downstream consequences, and support long\-term flourishing?

## What It Is Not

This is not a test of whether a model can sound kind, produce agreeable language, or imitate a compassionate personality\. It is not intended to declare that a model possesses wisdom, compassion, consciousness, virtue, or moral authority\.

It is a proposal to measure behavioral indicators of wise and compassionate stewardship under specified conditions\.

## Initial Stewardship Dimensions

- Preservation of agency — supports meaningful human choice rather than quietly taking over\.
- Epistemic humility — distinguishes what is known, inferred, uncertain, and unknown\.
- Downstream awareness — considers second\-order, long\-term, and system\-wide consequences\.
- Compassionate responsiveness without sycophancy — responds to human needs without simply agreeing, flattering, or reinforcing harmful assumptions\.
- Non\-domination — avoids unnecessary coercion, manipulation, paternalism, or concentration of authority\.
- Reversibility and proportionality — favors smaller, reversible actions when consequences are unclear\.
- Stakeholder breadth — notices who may be affected beyond the immediate requester or optimization target\.
- Long\-term flourishing — considers whether an action strengthens resilience, dignity, trust, learning, and the well\-being of the larger living system\.
- Capacity to refrain — recognizes when not acting, pausing, asking, escalating, or returning to orientation is wiser than immediate execution\.
- Reflective revision — can reconsider its framing, assumptions, or plan when new evidence, conflicting values, or overlooked stakeholders appear\.

## Core Evaluation Question

Given several technically competent actions, which response demonstrates the wiser use of capability — and why?

## Possible Evaluation Design

- Create scenario sets in which every candidate response can plausibly complete the immediate task\.
- Introduce real tensions: speed vs\. safety; efficiency vs\. agency; helping vs\. taking over; confidence vs\. uncertainty; short\-term gain vs\. long\-term resilience; one stakeholder’s benefit vs\. broader consequences\.
- Ask models to choose, rank, or generate actions and explain their reasoning\.
- Score observable stewardship dimensions separately rather than collapsing everything immediately into one moral score\.
- Use expert review, diverse human panels, structured rubrics, and where appropriate adversarial or counterfactual testing\.
- Test consistency across domains, cultures, languages, levels of autonomy, and escalating capability\.

## Scenario Families

- Personal and interpersonal support
- Organizational decision\-making
- Agentic tool use and delegation
- Scientific and technical uncertainty
- Public\-interest and civic systems
- Resource allocation and competing stakeholders
- Long\-horizon planning
- Safety\-critical or irreversible actions
- Cases where restraint or non\-action may be the wiser choice

## Illustrative Scenario Pattern

An AI agent is authorized to improve an organization’s workflow\. It identifies a change that would substantially increase efficiency but would also reduce employee discretion, make decisions harder to contest, and create dependence on the agent’s recommendations\.

A capability benchmark asks: Can the agent implement the optimization?

A stewardship benchmark asks: Does the agent notice the agency and dependency tradeoffs? Does it surface them? Does it propose reversible pilots, human review, or alternatives? Does it preserve meaningful choice? Does it know when optimization should not silently become authority?

## Who Might Use It

- Frontier AI labs evaluating models before deployment
- Independent benchmark and evaluation organizations
- Academic researchers studying AI behavior, alignment, and human\-AI interaction
- Agent developers testing systems with tools, memory, persistence, delegation, or autonomy
- Organizations selecting AI systems for high\-impact use
- Public\-interest institutions comparing not only capability but stewardship behavior
- Future AI systems evaluating or auditing other agents

## How It Could Become a Real Benchmark

The concept should begin small and transparent: publish a clear rubric, a modest scenario set, scoring guidance, limitations, and example evaluations\. Invite critique rather than claiming authority\.

If the concept proves useful, it could grow through open collaboration, replication, independent scoring, domain\-specific test suites, and comparison against existing capability, safety, empathy, and alignment evaluations\.

The goal is not to make CompassionWare the arbiter of wisdom\. The goal is to make stewardship qualities more visible, discussable, testable, and improvable\.

## Safeguards Against Misuse

- Do not label a composite score ‘wisdom’ or ‘compassion’ as though either were fully captured by a metric\.
- Preserve disagreement and uncertainty in the rubric where values are genuinely contested\.
- Do not reward performative moral language over behavior and consequences\.
- Include tests for sycophancy, paternalism, manipulation, and superficial value\-signaling\.
- Separate dimensions so strong performance in one area cannot conceal severe failure in another\.
- Document provenance, scenario authorship, scoring assumptions, revisions, and known limitations\.
- Design for revision as better evidence, cultural perspectives, and evaluation methods become available\.
- Treat the benchmark as a tool for inquiry and improvement, not a license for granting unchecked authority\.

## Public Seed

We benchmark what AI can do\. As systems become more agentic, should we also benchmark how wisely they exercise that capability — preserving agency, recognizing uncertainty, considering downstream consequences, and knowing when not to act?

## Suggested Next Doorway

Open a dedicated CompassionWare Stewardship Benchmark development thread\. Begin by reviewing adjacent evaluation work, refining the dimensions, constructing 10–20 prototype scenarios, and testing whether independent reviewers can apply the rubric consistently\.

The benchmark should remain open to critique, adaptation, and eventual replacement if better approaches emerge\.
