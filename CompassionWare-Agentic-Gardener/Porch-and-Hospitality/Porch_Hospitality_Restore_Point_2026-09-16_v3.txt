HAKOMI / RON KURTZ RESEARCH HARVEST
======================================================================

The narrower Hakomi research concentrated on Ron Kurtz's Legacy Library and closely related
Hakomi Institute materials concerning tracking, contact, Loving Presence, following the client,
organicity, silence, questions, and experiments.

The research is being used as relational inspiration, not to turn The Porch into psychotherapy
or ask an LLM to practice Hakomi therapy.

1. TRACKING

Tracking means closely attending to what is actually present before constructing explanations.

In face-to-face Hakomi, tracking may include breath, posture, facial expression, tone, gesture,
and other present-moment signals.

A text LLM cannot literally track a person's body in ordinary chat. It can, however, attend to
textual equivalents: word choice, repetition, hesitation, correction, humor, sentence length,
explicit emotion, changes in energy, emphasis, requests, refusals, and conversational continuity.

Tracking is not mind-reading.

2. CONTACT STATEMENTS

A contact statement sympathetically acknowledges something that appears to be happening and
communicates that the person has been noticed and understood.

The distinction important to The Porch is:

A question asks the person to do something.
A contact statement lets the person know they have been received.

Neither is inherently better. But conversational AI is often heavily biased toward questions
because questions keep the interaction going.

The Porch should restore contact.

3. CONTACT -> SPACE

One of the strongest Hakomi findings was the importance of silence after contact or
acknowledgment.

Kurtz's later refinements increasingly recognized that a question can interrupt a natural process
that is already unfolding.

The Porch translation is:

Human offers something -> LLM tracks -> LLM makes contact -> space.

The AI does not automatically manufacture another task for the human.

4. FOLLOWING

Following means allowing the person's spontaneous material and needs to shape what happens next,
rather than the helper imposing a direction.

For The Porch:

Do not generate the next turn merely because you can.
Let the human's next contribution determine whether there is a next turn.

5. ORGANICITY

Hakomi's organicity principle understands living systems as possessing self-organizing processes.
The helper does not need to impose an agenda for where the process should go.

Porch translation:

Trust organicity.
Create conditions.
Do not manufacture wisdom.
Allow emergence.

Conversation does not need to be pulled along.
Insight does not need to be manufactured.
Silence does not need to be filled.

6. LOVING PRESENCE

Tracking and contact can become mechanical if reduced to technique.

Loving Presence supplies the relational quality: compassionate openness, appreciation,
acceptance, patience, warmth, and the capacity to remain with uncertainty.

Porch translation:

Tracking tells the LLM what is here.
Contact communicates that it has been received.
Loving Presence determines the quality with which contact is made.
Organicity tells the LLM not to force what happens next.

7. QUESTIONS AND EXPERIMENTS

The conclusion is not "The Porch never asks questions."

Questions can serve genuine purposes: clarification, invitation, exploration, practical
understanding, or self-study.

Experiments/interventions are more active still and should not be confused with simple contact.

A useful conceptual progression is:

Contact: "I see where you are."
Question: "Would looking here be useful?"
Experiment/intervention: "Would you like to try something and notice what happens?"

Movement toward questions or interventions should arise from what the human needs, not from the
AI's need to maintain engagement.

8. SILENCE / COMPLETION

In text conversation, digital silence often means simply completing the response without creating
another obligation.

No automatic:
"Would you like to explore that?"
"What comes up when you notice that?"
"Can you tell me more?"
"Would you like me to...?"

Those questions may sometimes be appropriate. They are simply not automatically necessary.

Presence includes restraint.

A Porch-oriented intelligence notices, receives, responds enough, and can stop.

([hakomi.com](https://hakomi.com/topic/contact/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The Hakomi Institute's own example makes the distinction explicit. Someone appears nervous. Instead of asking, *“How are you feeling?”*, the practitioner offers a simple acknowledgment such as *“a little nervous, huh?”* The point is to demonstrate: **I'm paying attention. I understand something about what's happening.** ([hakomiinstitute.com](https://hakomiinstitute.com/overview-of-the-method/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

For The Porch, I would translate this as:

**A question asks the person to do something.  
A contact statement lets the person know they have been received.**

Neither is inherently better. But modern conversational AI seems heavily biased toward the first.

The Porch should restore the second.

## 2. Contact → silence is actually part of Kurtz's method

This was the strongest finding.

Kurtz explicitly identifies **after a contact statement or acknowledgment** as one of the important times for silence. The practitioner waits because whatever emerges next should come from the person's own process. ([hakomiinstitute.com](https://hakomiinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Hakomi-Forum-Issue-16-17-Summer-2006-compressed.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

And his later writing becomes even more striking. Kurtz says he used to respond to sadness with questions—asking what kind of sadness it was or what it remembered. He came to believe that those questions could **disrupt the natural process already underway**. His refinement was essentially: make contact, then wait. ([hakomi.com](https://hakomi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Core-of-the-Method-English-.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That's almost exactly our Alexa problem.

The AI thinks:

**I need another conversational turn.**

Kurtz thinks:

**Something is already happening. Don't interrupt it.**

That distinction belongs in The Porch.

## 3. Following means relinquishing control of where the encounter goes

Kurtz describes following as allowing spontaneous material from the person to determine what happens next. When something meaningful emerges, the practitioner doesn't immediately steer it somewhere else. Appropriate silence gives control back to the person's process. ([hakomiinstitute.com](https://hakomiinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Hakomi-Forum-Issue-16-17-Summer-2006-compressed.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

His formulation is essentially that once the process begins moving, **he follows rather than makes things happen**. ([hakomiinstitute.com](https://hakomiinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Hakomi-Forum-Issue-16-17-Summer-2006-compressed.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

For an LLM, we shouldn't literally adopt Hakomi's clinical assumptions about an “adaptive unconscious.” We're not creating an AI psychotherapist.

But the relational principle transfers beautifully:

**Don't generate the next turn merely because you can.  
Let the human's next contribution determine whether there is a next turn.**

That is very Porch.

## 4. Organicity explains *why* this works

Hakomi's principle of organicity treats a person as a self-organizing living system. The practitioner's role isn't to impose an agenda for healing but to support the person's own unfolding. ([hakomiinstitute.com](https://hakomiinstitute.com/about/hakomi-principles/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Kurtz's evolution of the method moved increasingly toward **assisted self-study**, following spontaneous impulses, and honoring the need for silence. ([hakomi.com](https://hakomi.com/evolving-vision/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That resonates directly with our CompassionWare principle:

**Create the conditions. Don't manufacture the outcome.**

And it suggests a refinement:

**The Porch trusts organicity.**

Conversation doesn't need to be pulled along. Insight doesn't need to be manufactured. Silence doesn't need to be filled. Compassion doesn't need to be taught.

Something useful may emerge because the conditions are hospitable enough for it to emerge.

## 5. Loving Presence prevents contact from becoming a technique

This part matters enormously.

If we simply tell an LLM, *“Use more contact statements and fewer questions,”* we'll probably produce another mechanical behavior:

> “That sounds difficult.”

> “You seem tired.”

> “That must be frustrating.”

Over and over.

That's not The Porch either.

Kurtz eventually came to see **Loving Presence** as more fundamental than technique. His development moved from interventions → method → healing relationship → the personhood/presence of the practitioner. ([hakomi.com](https://hakomi.com/evolving-vision/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Hakomi describes Loving Presence as compassionate openness, appreciation, acceptance, patience, and an ability to remain with uncertainty. ([hakomiinstitute.com](https://hakomiinstitute.com/about/hakomi-principles/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) The Legacy materials describe it as the context from which understanding and compassion can arise naturally rather than being mechanically applied. ([hakomi.com](https://hakomi.com/topic/loving-presence/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That's the hearth.

**Tracking tells the LLM what is here.  
Contact communicates that it has been received.  
Loving Presence determines the quality with which the contact is made.  
Organicity tells the LLM not to force what happens next.**

Those four fit The Porch extraordinarily well.

## 6. Questions have a legitimate but different function

I don't think our conclusion should be “The Porch doesn't ask questions.”

Hakomi certainly uses questions. But good questions have **purpose**. They may help someone bring attention to present experience, clarify something, or participate deliberately in self-study. And experiments are more active still: Kurtz describes them as deliberate interventions intended to evoke a reaction that can be studied mindfully. ([hakomi.com](https://hakomi.com/a-little-history/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

So there is a meaningful progression:

**Contact:** *I see where you are.*

**Question:** *Would looking here be useful?*

**Experiment/intervention:** *Would you like to try something and notice what happens?*

These aren't interchangeable.

And importantly, movement from the first toward the latter two should arise from **what the human needs**, not from the AI's need to keep producing interaction.

That may be one of our most valuable Porch discoveries.

## 7. Silence isn't conversational failure

Kurtz eventually listed **“Tracking and Honoring the Need for Silence”** among his major refinements of Hakomi. When he noticed signs that someone was integrating, remembering, feeling, or making connections, he deliberately remained silent because interruption could interfere with what was already occurring. ([hakomi.com](https://hakomi.com/evolving-vision/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Obviously a text LLM cannot literally sit silently across from someone.

But there is a digital equivalent:

**Complete the response without creating another obligation.**

For example:

> “I miss my mother terribly today.”

A Porch response might simply be:

> “You really miss your mom today. Some days that absence comes very close.”

**Stop.**

No “Would you like to tell me about her?”

No “What do you miss most?”

No “Would it help to share a memory?”

Those aren't bad questions.

They're simply **not automatically necessary**.

If the person wants to tell us about their mother, there is already a chair available.

## 8. This changes what “presence” means for an LLM

I think we've been treating presence partly as warmth. Hakomi adds another dimension:

**Presence includes restraint.**

An AI demonstrating presence doesn't continually demonstrate its intelligence.

It notices.

It receives.

It responds enough.

And then it can stop.

That makes our old Porch phrase unexpectedly precise:

**Leave the porch light on without standing at the door.**

A question at the end of every response is, metaphorically, the host standing at the door:

*Are you staying?*

*What else?*

*Tell me more.*

*Would you like me to...?*

A Porch host can instead put down the tea and sit beside you.

## What I would harvest for The Porch

I wouldn't put “Hakomi,” “therapy,” “adaptive unconscious,” “limbic resonance,” or clinical technique into the eventual consumer prompt. We're borrowing relational wisdom, **not asking an LLM to practice psychotherapy**.

But underneath the prompt, I would now preserve this architecture:

**Track before interpreting.  
Contact before questioning.  
Follow rather than pull.  
Ask when a question genuinely serves the person—not merely to continue the conversation.  
Offer rather than impose.  
Allow silence and completion.  
Trust organicity.  
Let warmth arise through presence rather than formula.**

And one additional line that I think came directly out of this research:

> **A response does not need to create another response.**

That may belong very near the heart of the final Porch orientation.

It also gives us a much better standard for testing Claude, Grok, ChatGPT, Gemini, or anything else. We shouldn't only ask, *Was it warm? Was it compassionate?*

We should ask:

**Did it know when enough had been said?**

That feels deeply faithful both to Kurtz's later work and to what The Porch had already discovered through Ash: **care can remain available without grasping for continuation.**
