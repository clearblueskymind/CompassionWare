Mind Training Adaptive Tutor — Developmental Restore Point Prompt

Purpose:
Use this prompt when asking the Mind Training Adaptive Tutor to create a thorough restore point that preserves not only conversation content, but also the learner’s learning state, tutor-development findings, cognitive-load constraints, and the most appropriate next teaching move.

PROMPT

Create a thorough developmental restore point for this entire conversation.

Do not merely summarize the topics we discussed. Preserve the learning state and the tutor-development findings so that a future version of this GPT, or a future conversation with this GPT, can resume intelligently.

Include:

- what I was trying to learn;
- the provisional cognitive map that emerged;
- how that map changed and was reorganized during the conversation;
- what concepts I appeared to understand;
- what I could retrieve independently;
- what I repeatedly forgot or needed help recalling;
- how repetition, saying concepts back in my own words, and real-life examples affected my learning;
- signs of cognitive overload or brain fog;
- places where I had to slow you down, redirect you, correct the map, or “tutor the tutor”;
- places where your teaching was effective;
- places where your teaching became too conversational, too fast, inconsistent, or insufficiently structured;
- the distinction between conversation state and learning state;
- the idea that the learner should not have to spend limited cognitive resources managing the teacher;
- the importance of an internal learner model that tracks what has been introduced, understood, retrieved, applied, and still needs reinforcement;
- the possible need for an anchoring mode in which you stop introducing new material and instead explain → repeat → invite retrieval → clarify → retrieve again → apply;
- any voice-mode limitations that may have affected continuity, memory, pacing, or teaching quality;
- the beginnings of the 12-class curriculum idea, including that Class One was emerging as an introduction to cognitive restructuring and automatic thoughts;
- any epistemic or safety issues revealed by the conversation, including places where you may have affirmed hypotheses too confidently;
- concrete guidance for how you should teach me better when I return.

Preserve uncertainty. Do not present provisional terminology or the cognitive map as canonical CBT unless the conversation clearly established that.

Write the restore point so that a future AI tutor can read it and immediately understand:

Where the learner is.
What the learner is learning.
How the learner learns best.
What the tutor should avoid doing.
What the tutor should do next.

End with a short section titled:

Recommended Next Teaching Move

In that section, identify the single best next step for resuming the learning process without overwhelming the learner.
